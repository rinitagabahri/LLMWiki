# Build the Team, or Change It: Drexler/Sibbet vs. The Team Model

**Faced with the same complaint — "my team is underperforming" — the two team models point in opposite directions: work the people you have, or judge whether they're the right people. The clash resolves on one diagnosis: is the gap developmental or a real capability/fit mismatch?**

## The clash

| | [[drexler-sibbet-team-performance]] | [[team-model]] |
|---|---|---|
| **Premise** | Any group *becomes* a team by working the stages | A team either *has* the qualities the job needs, or it doesn't |
| **A shortfall is…** | Developmental — a stage not yet resolved | A selection problem — a missing quality |
| **The move** | Work the process; the leader goes first on trust | Judge honestly; develop, motivate, or change the lineup |
| **Then…** | Keep building through Renewal | Delegate — and stop meddling (Roosevelt) |
| **Origin** | Drexler & Sibbet / The Grove | The Decision Book, Ch.47 (Roosevelt anchor) |

## Why they seem to contradict

Drexler/Sibbet says: *don't blame the people — work the process.* A group that looks weak is usually just stuck at an unresolved stage (trust not built, goals not clear), and the fix is to work through it, with the leader daring the first step. The Team Model says: *first ask whether they're the right people at all.* Rate the team on the qualities the job demands; if a quality is simply absent, no amount of stage-work conjures it — you develop it, motivate it, or bring in someone who has it. One says fix the team you have; the other says be willing to fix the roster.

## How they reconcile — on the diagnosis

The deciding variable is **what kind of gap you're actually looking at**:

- **Developmental gap** (the people could do it, but haven't gelled — low trust, fuzzy goals, no shared commitment) → **Drexler/Sibbet.** Work the stages; going straight to "wrong people" would throw away a team that just needs building.
- **Capability / fit gap** (a quality the job genuinely requires is missing from the group, and no stage resolves it) → **The Team Model.** Name the missing quality and act on it — train, re-motivate, or restaff — then delegate.

So the honest first step is the Team Model's rating; only once you've ruled out a true capability gap does Drexler/Sibbet's "work the process, don't blame the people" become the right advice rather than an excuse. Misdiagnose it and you either churn good people who only needed building, or endlessly "build" a team that structurally can't do the job.

## The synthesis

Rate first, then build. Use the Team Model to make the uncomfortable capability call honestly — and to check the shortfall isn't just your own failure to delegate. If the people are right, switch to Drexler/Sibbet and work the stages, leader first. **Judging the team and developing the team aren't rivals; they're the order of operations — and both end in the same place: pick well, then get out of the way.**
