# The Chasm vs. The Hype Cycle

**Two curves that look alike and get confused constantly — but one tracks how *excited* people are, the other tracks who's *actually adopting*. A product can be climbing out of the hype trough while still stuck at the chasm, or vice versa.**

## The clash

| | [[hype-cycle]] | [[the-chasm-model]] |
|---|---|---|
| **What the curve measures** | *Expectations / sentiment* over time | *Adopter segments / real behaviour* |
| **The danger zone** | Trough of Disillusionment (excitement collapses) | The chasm (early adopters → early majority gap) |
| **What kills a product there** | Everyone loses interest before real value emerges | Pragmatists won't buy without peer proof |
| **Origin** | Gartner | Geoffrey Moore / Everett Rogers |

## Why they're not the same curve

The most common mistake is reading **early-adopter enthusiasm as mainstream traction** — treating loud sentiment as proof of adoption. The two curves separate exactly there:

- The **Hype Cycle** is about *feelings in the crowd*: a technology is overhyped at the peak, written off in the trough, and only vindicated on the slope. It answers *"is the excitement real or noise?"*
- The **Chasm** is about *who is buying*: momentum with visionaries doesn't transfer to pragmatists, because they buy for opposite reasons. It answers *"has this crossed from the few to the many?"*

They can point in opposite directions at once. A product can be **climbing the Slope of Enlightenment** (hype recovering, real use cases proven) yet still **stuck at the chasm** (no pragmatist references, adoption flat) — genuinely useful, quietly ignored. Or it can be **deep in the hype trough** (sentiment brutal) while a niche beachhead is *already crossing the chasm* underneath the bad press.

## When to reach for which

- **Reach for the Hype Cycle** to time *attention*: don't buy at the peak or write off at the trough; judge fundamentals, not the mood.
- **Reach for the Chasm** to plan *adoption*: don't mistake early fans for a market; win one narrow niche before going broad.
- **The trap both warn against, from different sides:** betting on a thing because it's *loud* (hype) rather than because pragmatists are *actually adopting* it (chasm). Sentiment and behaviour are not the same signal.

## The synthesis

Use them as two gauges on the same dashboard. The Hype Cycle tells you whether the *conversation* about a new thing is inflated or depressed; the Chasm tells you whether *usage* has jumped the gap from enthusiasts to the mainstream. The mature read watches both: **discount the mood, and track the adoption underneath it.**
