# Choosing Cooperation: Prisoner's Dilemma vs. Conflict Resolution

**Both prize the same prize — the win/win — but they give opposite verdicts on whether you can just *choose* it. The whole clash resolves on one variable: is the game one-shot or repeated?**

## The clash

| | [[conflict-resolution-model]] | [[prisoners-dilemma]] |
|---|---|---|
| **Win/win is…** | A response you can **choose** by surfacing shared interests | A structurally **unstable** outcome the incentives fight against |
| **The lever** | Communication — talk until interests are on the table | Repetition — the shadow of the future |
| **In a one-shot** | Still reachable if both sides open up | Won't hold: defection dominates, however skilled you are |
| **The fix** | Reach consensus in the moment | Change the game *into* a repeated one (build a future, a reputation) |
| **Origin** | The Decision Book, Ch.12 | Flood & Dresher, RAND 1950; Axelrod 1984 |

## Why they seem to contradict

Conflict Resolution treats consensus as the elegant response you *pick* — get past positions, find the shared interest, and win/win is on the table. The Prisoner's Dilemma says: in a one-shot game between self-interested parties, mutual cooperation is structurally unstable no matter how skilled the conversation, because defecting dominates whatever the other does. One says "choose it"; the other says "you can't just choose it." Same prize, opposite advice.

## How they reconcile — on the single variable of repetition

- **Ongoing relationship where you can talk** → reach for **Conflict Resolution.** Communication and a shared future are already present, so consensus really is a response you can choose; PD's warning is satisfied because the game repeats.
- **One-shot, incentive-driven standoff where you can't coordinate** → **PD warns the consensus won't hold.** The move isn't to "choose" cooperation in the moment — it's to *change the game*: create a future, a reputation, a reason to meet again, so tomorrow's retaliation makes today's cooperation rational.

So they're two layers: Conflict Resolution is the *skill* of steering a live conversation toward win/win; the Prisoner's Dilemma is the *precondition* that tells you whether that skill can hold — and, when it can't, what to fix (the structure, not the sentiment).

## The synthesis

When there's a shared future and a channel to talk, use Conflict Resolution and choose consensus. When there isn't, don't trust a handshake — build the repetition first (a contract, a reputation, an ongoing relationship), then the win/win becomes something you can actually reach for. **Consensus is a choice only once the game rewards it.**
