## Models

- [[eisenhower-matrix]] — Prioritize tasks by distinguishing urgent from important (Chapter 1)
- [[swot-analysis]] — Map your position before a big decision (Chapter 2)
- [[bcg-box]] — Decide where to invest resources across multiple bets (Chapter 3)
- [[project-portfolio-matrix]] — Evaluate projects by vision alignment and learning potential (Chapter 4)
- [[john-whitmore-mode]] — Pressure-test whether you're chasing the right goal (GROW) (Chapter 5)
- [[rubber-band-model]] — Resolve a dilemma by separating values-pulls from fear-pulls (Chapter 6)
- [[feedback-model]] — Decide what to do with criticism: change, keep, or reject (Chapter 7)
- [[family-tree-model]] — Read the shape of your network: diversification, concentration risk, and which boughs could break (Chapter 8)
- [[morphological-box-scamper]] — Structured creativity: vary the parts of a problem to generate new ideas (Chapter 9)
- [[esquire-gift-model]] — Decide who to give gifts to, when, and how much (Chapter 10)
- [[consequences-model]] — Decide promptly with minimal information; consequences are unforecastable anyway (Chapter 11)
- [[conflict-resolution-model]] — Choose your response in a conflict: flight, fight, give up, evade, compromise, or consensus (Chapter 12)
- [[crossroads-model]] — At a crossroads, name all six possible roads — don't settle for the sensible one by default (Chapter 13)
- [[flow-model]] — Diagnose why you're stuck (anxiety or boredom) and calibrate the challenge/skill balance to reach flow (Chapter 14)
- [[johari-window]] — Map what you and others know about you to find blind spots and decide what to reveal (Chapter 15)
- [[cognitive-dissonance-model]] — When actions contradict beliefs, you resolve the tension by changing behaviour or rationalising it away (Chapter 16)
- [[music-matrix]] — What your taste in music says about you: mapped on mainstream/avant-garde and authentic/designer axes (Chapter 17)
- [[unimaginable-model]] — Surface the beliefs driving you that you cannot prove, don't fully understand, or have no evidence for (Chapter 18)
- [[uffe-elbaek-model]] — See yourself from four angles: how you see yourself, want to see yourself, how others see you, how others want to see you (Chapter 19)
- [[fashion-model]] — How you dress maps to a style zone (formal/casual × old school/hip) that signals your identity to others (Chapter 20)
- [[energy-model]] — Where your mental energy goes (past/present/future) reveals your default type: memory driven, dream driven, or reality driven (Chapter 21)
- [[supermemo-model]] — Retain everything you learn by reviewing at spaced intervals: day 1, 10, 30, 60 — retrievability vs. stability (Chapter 22)
- [[political-compass]] — Locate a political position on two axes — economic (left/right) and social (authoritarian/libertarian) — not just one line (Chapter 23)
- [[personal-performance-model]] — Rate your tasks each evening for 3 weeks on have-to / able-to / want-to; a moving shape means growth, a flat one means you're stuck (Chapter 24)
- [[making-of-model]] — Reconstruct a past period like a "making-of" documentary (people, goals, successes, obstacles, lessons) to read where you're heading (Chapter 25)
- [[personal-potential-trap]] — Expectations (yours and others') climb faster than your achievements; the widening gap — not your real success — is what makes you feel never enough (Chapter 26)
- [[hype-cycle]] — Every technology follows the same curve (trigger → peak → trough → slope → plateau); the "next big thing" and the "flop" are often the same thing at different points (Chapter 27)
- [[subtle-signals-model]] — Map your work conversations by frequency and effect; healthy working life means shifting time toward the talks that leave you better off (Chapter 28)
- [[network-target-model]] — Plot everyone you know in a target: four quadrants (family/friends/colleagues/acquaintances) × frequency rings (daily→yearly); shows where your time really goes and how like-you your inner circle is (Chapter 29)
- [[superficial-knowledge-model]] — A wry 2×2 (easy/difficult × embarrassing/impressive) for deciding what's worth learning; the "easy + impressive" corner buys the most impression for the least effort (Chapter 30)
- [[swiss-cheese-model]] — Big failures happen when the holes in several layers of defence (people, technical, organisational, outside) line up; blame the system, not just the last person (Chapter 31)
- [[maslow-pyramid]] — A five-level hierarchy of needs that separates what you actually *need* (the lower rungs) from what you *want* (the top); you can't reach the peak while the base is crumbling (Chapter 32)
- [[thinking-outside-the-box]] — The nine-dot puzzle: the wall blocking a brilliant idea is usually a boundary you drew yourself; ideas live in the "innovative field" where new/old and chaos/order overlap (Chapter 33)
- [[sinus-milieu-bourdieu]] — Two maps of "where you belong": the Sinus Milieu groups a society by status × values, and Bourdieu plots you by economic vs. cultural capital — chart yourself, your parents, and your ideal to see your trajectory (Chapter 34)
- [[double-loop-learning]] — Learn from a mistake by questioning the *assumptions and goals* behind the action, not just correcting the action; second-order observation turned on your own frame (Chapter 35)
- [[appreciative-inquiry]] — The "AI Model": improve by building on what already works, not fixing what's broken — because change follows attention (Chapter 36)
- [[small-world-model]] — Any two people are ~six handshakes apart; your reach is defined not by what you can do but by who you know — and the weak ties (acquaintances) bridge you furthest (Chapter 37)
- [[pareto-principle]] — The 80/20 rule: ~80% of results come from ~20% of inputs; effort is lopsided, not linear, so find the vital few and do them first (Chapter 38)
- [[long-tail-model]] — The internet flips Pareto: when shelf space is infinite and distribution ~free, the many niches (the "long tail") in aggregate can out-sell the few best-sellers — "sell less of more" (Chapter 39)
- [[monte-carlo-simulation]] — Too many uncertain variables for one exact answer, so run it thousands of times with random inputs and read the distribution — a range with odds, not a single certainty (Chapter 40)
- [[black-swan-model]] — Rare, high-impact events are unpredictable outliers; experience (induction from the past) can't foresee them and only rationalises them as "obvious" in hindsight — so build for robustness, not forecasts (Chapter 41)
- [[the-chasm-model]] — How a new idea spreads through a population (the five adopter groups) and where most die: the "chasm" between novelty-loving early adopters and the proof-demanding majority — cross it by dominating one niche beachhead first (Chapter 42)
- [[black-box-model]] — You know what goes into a system and what comes out, but not how it works inside; as the world grows more complex, trust quietly replaces genuine understanding — so the real question becomes whether the source is worthy of that trust (Chapter 43)
- [[status-model]] — Read someone's real status from how they earned their money (earned ↔ inherited) × how they use it (egoistic ↔ altruistic); the "winner" is the one who built it *and* gives it back (Chapter 44)
- [[prisoners-dilemma]] — Two people each acting in rational self-interest end up worse off than if they'd trusted each other; trust becomes the rational move once the game *repeats* (shadow of the future, tit-for-tat) (Chapter 45)
- [[drexler-sibbet-team-performance]] — Turn a group into a team by working through seven stages in order (Orientation → Trust → Goals → Commitment → Implementation → High Performance → Renewal); the team only moves forward when someone dares the first step, so the leader should be first to make mistakes (Chapter 46)
- [[team-model]] — Is your team up to the job? Rate it low-to-high on the qualities every strong team needs (scorer, tireless worker, tackler, strong nerves, motivator); a team of stars isn't a star team — find the missing quality, then (Roosevelt) pick good people and delegate without meddling (Chapter 47)
- [[gap-in-the-market-model]] — Plot every rival in a 3D cube (cost-effectiveness × prestige × awareness) and the conspicuous empty region is where a bankable idea might sit — but a gap is necessary, not sufficient: a gap in the market isn't the same as a market in the gap, so always ask *why* it's empty (Chapter 48)
- [[hersey-blanchard-model]] — Situational leadership: there's no single best style — read each employee's readiness (competence + commitment) on the task and match it (instruct the keen novice, coach the disillusioned learner, support the capable-but-unsure, delegate to the self-reliant expert), shifting as they grow (Chapter 49)
- [[role-playing-model]] — Change your own point of view on demand: put on a different thinking "hat" (de Bono — facts, feelings, caution, optimism, creativity, process) or step into a different team role (Belbin's nine, in three families) — a viewpoint is a role you can choose, so deliberately wear the one you'd normally avoid (Chapter 50)
- [[result-optimisation-model]] — *(bonus model)* Don't optimise a result in one pass: run gather → consolidate → implement three times, so each round surfaces what the last missed — including the un-rehearsed step that (Murphy's Law) would otherwise break just before the deadline

## Comparisons

- [[pareto-vs-long-tail]] — Focus on the vital few, or keep the many? The two reconcile on one variable — the cost of serving the tail (effort/attention vs. inventory/reach)
- [[black-swan-vs-monte-carlo]] — Both reason about uncertain futures, but split on whether the model itself can be trusted: Monte Carlo samples *known unknowns* inside a distribution; the Black Swan is the *unknown unknown* outside it
- [[chasm-vs-hype-cycle]] — Two curves that look alike but measure different things: the Hype Cycle tracks *expectations/sentiment*, the Chasm tracks *who is actually adopting* — a product can climb the hype slope while still stuck at the chasm
- [[choosing-cooperation]] — Prisoner's Dilemma vs. Conflict Resolution: both want the win/win, but split on whether you can just *choose* it — resolves on one variable, whether the game is one-shot or repeated
- [[build-or-staff-the-team]] — Drexler/Sibbet vs. The Team Model: on the same "my team is underperforming," build the people you have or change the roster — resolves on whether the gap is developmental or a real capability/fit mismatch
- [[coach-or-adapt]] — GROW vs. Hersey-Blanchard: on the same "how do I develop this person," always coach or match the style — resolves on the person's readiness (coaching fits only the disillusioned learner, not the novice or the expert)
- [[iterate-or-commit]] — Consequences vs. Result Optimisation: on the same "how much to refine before I commit," decide fast or run three rounds first — resolves on the reversibility of a late failure (cheap to undo → commit now; catastrophic at the deadline → iterate and harden)
