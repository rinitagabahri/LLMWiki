# The AI Model (Appreciative Inquiry)

**Improve things by building on what already works — not by hunting down what's broken.** *(David Cooperrider & Suresh Srivastva, Case Western Reserve.)*

## Problem-Focus vs. Appreciative-Focus

The traditional **deficit** approach runs: *find the problem → dig for root causes → engineer a fix → roll it out.* It treats the situation as broken and points all attention at the gaps. Appreciative Inquiry **inverts** this: *appreciate what already works → imagine more of it → design how to get there → deliver and sustain.* One starts from what's missing; the other from what's alive. The claim isn't that problems don't matter — it's that studying strengths generates more energy and better change than cataloguing faults.

## Four Ways People React to an Idea

When someone floats an idea, your response lands in one of four quadrants — set by whether you're **positive or negative** about it (columns) and whether you add something **constructive or destructive** (rows):

| | **Negative** | **Positive** |
|---|---|---|
| **Constructive** | 🧑‍🏫 **The Schoolteacher** — *"No, the idea's not good, because…"* | ✅ **The AI Thinker** — *"Yes, and we could also…"* |
| **Destructive** | 🚫 **The Dictator** — *"No."* | 🙅 **The Fault-Finder** — *"The idea's good, but…"* |

- **The AI thinker** is the only one who *builds* — accepts the idea and adds to it. This is Appreciative Inquiry in a single sentence: *"Yes, and…"*.
- **The Fault-finder** sounds supportive ("good, but…") yet the "but" deletes everything before it — positive on the surface, destructive underneath.
- **The Schoolteacher** at least reasons ("no, because…"), so the idea's owner learns something — negative, but constructive.
- **The Dictator** just kills it — no energy, no reason, nothing to build on.

## When to Use It

- Morale is low and the team is stuck in blame or fault-finding — you need energy, not another problem list
- Kicking off change or a retrospective, and you want to build on what works rather than start from a deficit
- A relationship or collaboration has drifted into criticism — reframe around what's going right
- Setting vision or strategy: anchor the future in demonstrated strengths, not gaps to close

## Key Insight

Whatever question you keep asking, you get more of. Point the inquiry at strengths and you build momentum from real successes; point it only at faults and you get a thorough map of everything wrong but little energy to move. **Change follows attention** — so choose the topic of inquiry deliberately.

## Example

*Iris runs a support team whose satisfaction scores are slipping.*

- **Problem-focus:** audit every failed ticket to find what's broken. Useful data — but it leaves the team defensive and drained.
- **Appreciative-focus:** she asks instead, *"Think of a time a customer left delighted — what did we do?"* The stories cluster around a few habits: fast first replies and personal follow-ups. From there the team **dreams** up a "delight standard", **designs** it into their workflow, and **commits** to sustaining it.

Same slipping metric — but the appreciative frame gave them a direction pulled from their own best work, not a checklist of failures.
