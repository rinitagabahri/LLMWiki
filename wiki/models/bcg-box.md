# BCG Box

**Decide where to invest your limited resources across multiple bets.**

|  | **High Current Return** | **Low Current Return** |
|---|---|---|
| **High Growth** | ⭐ **Stars** — invest more | ❓ **Question Marks** — decide: invest or cut |
| **Low Growth** | 🐄 **Cash Cows** — maintain, don't over-invest | 🐕 **Dogs** — cut your losses |

## When to Use It

- You have multiple things competing for your time/money/energy and need to choose
- You're wondering whether to double down or cut something loose
- Quarterly review of where your resources are actually going
- Any "portfolio" decision — financial, career, projects, habits

## Key Insight

Not everything deserves equal investment. Cash cows fund your stars. Question marks need a deadline — either they become stars or they get cut. Dogs are sunk costs you need to let go of. The mistake most people make: over-investing in dogs out of loyalty or sunk cost, while starving their stars.

## Example

*Amara runs a small coffee roastery and can't fund every line at once. She sorts the four on the box:*

| Line | Quadrant | Move |
|---|---|---|
| **Subscription boxes** | ⭐ Star | Fast-growing *and* already profitable — pour marketing and roasting capacity here |
| **Wholesale to local cafés** | 🐄 Cash Cow | Flat but reliable margin — keep it running lean; it funds everything else |
| **Cold-brew cans** | ❓ Question Mark | Booming category, still losing money — give it one season and a clear target, then double down or cut |
| **Branded mugs & merch** | 🐕 Dog | Low sales, no growth, cash tied up in unsold stock — discontinue |

*The trap she nearly fell into: the mugs felt "part of the brand," so she kept reordering them out of sentiment — textbook over-investing in a Dog while the subscription Star was starved of capacity. The box makes the reallocation obvious: the cash cow funds the star, the question mark gets a deadline, and the dog goes.*

## Related Models

- [[eisenhower-matrix]] — Both are 2×2 grids for sorting. Eisenhower sorts *tasks by urgency/importance*; BCG sorts *investments by growth/return*.
- [[swot-analysis]] — SWOT evaluates one thing deeply; BCG compares multiple things against each other.
- [[long-tail-model]] — complicates the "cut your dogs" rule: a niche item looks like a 🐕 Dog on its own, but in aggregate — at near-zero online inventory cost — the tail can behave like a 🐄 Cash Cow
- [[family-tree-model]] — both are diversification frameworks where concentration is risk: BCG for business bets, Family Tree for your social/professional network
- [[project-portfolio-matrix]] — both are 2×2 portfolio matrices: BCG sorts by growth vs. return, that one by learning vs. vision alignment
