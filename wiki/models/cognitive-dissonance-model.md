# The Cognitive Dissonance Model

**When your actions contradict your beliefs, your mind feels discomfort — and will do anything to resolve it.**

*Term coined by psychologist Leon Festinger.*

## The Tension

```
Belief:  "Smoking is unhealthy and I know it."
Action:  Smokes anyway.
         ↕ dissonance — the gap creates mental discomfort
```

The mind can't hold a contradiction comfortably. It needs to resolve it — one way or another.

## The Two Exits

| Exit                     | What you do                                       | Example                                                                  |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Change the behaviour** | Close the gap by acting in line with your beliefs | Quit smoking or cut down |
| **Change the attitude**  | Reframe the belief to justify the behaviour       | "My grandfather smoked and lived to 90" / "I could get hit by a bus tomorrow anyway" |

Most people take the second exit — it's easier. The model makes you notice when you're doing it.

## When to Use It

- You catch yourself rationalising a behaviour you know is wrong
- You feel vague guilt or discomfort about something you're doing
- Someone points out a contradiction between what you say and what you do
- You want to audit whether your stated values actually show up in your actions

## Key Insight

Rationalisation is cognitive dissonance in disguise. When you hear yourself building a case for why the inconsistency doesn't count — "it's different because...", "just this once...", "at my scale it doesn't matter..." — that's the exit sign for the wrong door. The discomfort is the signal. The question is which exit you take.

## Example

*You tell people you care deeply about climate change. But you fly for short trips, leave the car running, and haven't changed a single habit. The dissonance builds.*

Two ways out:
- **Change behaviour:** book the train, cut one flight a year, make it visible to yourself. The action now matches the belief.
- **Change attitude:** "Individual action is a myth pushed by corporations to shift blame — systemic change is what matters." The belief now justifies the action. The discomfort disappears — but nothing changes.

The model doesn't tell you which exit is right. It tells you to be honest about which one you're taking.
