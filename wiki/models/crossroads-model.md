# The Crossroads Model

**At a crossroads most people weigh only two roads — naming all six reveals the one you actually want.**

Inspired by the **Personal Compass**, developed by San Francisco consulting agency **The Grove**.

```
              🌟 The road that beckons
                       │
   🏡 Safe   ←─────  YOU  ─────→  🦄 Wildest dream
                       │
              🧭 The sensible road
               🚪 Not travelled
               🔁 Already been on
```

## The Six Roads

| # | Road | What pulls you toward it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 🌟 **The road that beckons** | Genuine curiosity, energy, "this calls to me" |
| 2 | 🦄 **The road in my wildest dreams** | Pure imagination — *what if money/fear didn't exist?* |
| 3 | 🧭 **The road that seems most sensible** | Logic, advice from others, the "responsible" choice |
| 4 | 🚪 **The road not travelled** | A path you passed up before — a second chance |
| 5 | 🔁 **The road I've already been on** | Doing more of what you know |
| 6 | 🏡 **The road back to a safe place** | Returning to comfort, home, the familiar |

## The Five Compass Questions (the setup)

Before naming the roads, get yourself onto paper with these five:

1. **Where have I come from?** — the chapters that shaped you
2. **What is really important to me?** — values, non-negotiables
3. **Which people are important to me?** — whose presence and opinion matter
4. **Who is hindering me?** — people, expectations, or commitments holding you back
5. **What am I afraid of?** — the fears running silently in the background

## When to Use It

- A major life inflection point (end of a chapter, big birthday, after a loss, breakup, or job change)
- Year-end reflection or a planned sabbatical
- When "what next?" feels genuinely open and you can't articulate a goal yet
- When you're busy but not directed — moving without choosing

## Key Insight

**Most people only seriously consider roads 3 (sensible) and 5 (more of the same).** The other four get filtered out by adulthood, social expectations, fear, or sunk cost. Naming all six gives them equal seating at the table — and the *real* answer is often one of the four you'd usually ignore. The wildest-dream road usually contains the real desire; the road not travelled is where regret hides; the safe road is where exhaustion sends you. The model doesn't tell you the answer — **it exposes which road you've been ignoring.**

## Example — End of a 5-year work chapter

Someone hits the 5-year mark at their company. The current role feels finished, but the next step isn't obvious.

After running the 5 compass questions, they sketch the 6 roads:

- 🌟 **The road that beckons:** A specific role at a smaller, mission-driven company. Genuine spark when thinking about it.
- 🦄 **Wildest dream:** Take a year off, travel, write the book they've been talking about for years. Financially scary, emotionally clarifying.
- 🧭 **Most sensible:** Stay one more year for the next promotion, then reassess.
- 🚪 **Road not travelled:** Reconnect with the job offer they turned down 3 years ago.
- 🔁 **Already been on:** Move laterally inside the same company — same role, different team.
- 🏡 **Road back to safety:** Move home, take a quieter local role, reset.

**What the exercise reveals:**
- They've been *telling themselves* road 3 (one more year for the promotion).
- The actual energy sits with road 1 (the smaller mission-driven company).
- Road 2 (wildest dream) is whispering they need a real break before any next chapter — regardless of which one they pick.
- Road 5 (lateral move) is what they'd default to out of inertia.

**Payoff:** "What next?" becomes visible. The decision isn't made yet — but the *honest set of options* is now on the table, including the ones they'd have filtered out without the model.

## Related Models

- [[john-whitmore-mode]] — Whitmore sharpens a *goal you already have*. Crossroads helps you *find* the goal in the first place. Use Crossroads first, then GROW.
- [[swot-analysis]] — SWOT maps your external strategic position; Crossroads maps your internal compass. They complement each other.
- [[family-tree-model]] — The compass question *"Which people are important?"* overlaps with Family Tree's structural view of your network.
- [[rubber-band-model]] — The compass question *"What am I afraid of?"* surfaces the fear-pulls that Rubber Band then helps you discount.
- [[maslow-pyramid]] — the safe road answers deficiency needs, the wildest-dream road answers self-actualisation; Maslow labels which need each road serves.
- [[thinking-outside-the-box]] — the six-roads filter is a self-imposed box; both put the options you'd never normally consider back on the table.
- [[role-playing-model]] — same anti-default discipline for thinking modes: name all six hats and deliberately wear the one you habitually skip, just as Crossroads names the roads you filter out.
- [[changing-your-viewpoint]] — theme: naming all the roads is one move in the larger discipline of deliberately changing your own point of view.
- [[strategic-self-assessment]] — theme: Crossroads opens the option set the assessment sequence then sharpens
