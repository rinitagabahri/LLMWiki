# The Drexler/Sibbet Team Performance Model

**A group only becomes a team by working through seven stages in order — and it moves forward only when someone dares the first step, which is why the leader should be the first to make mistakes.**

Each stage is a question every member is quietly asking. Answer them in order; skipped ones come back as friction.

```
  CREATING (get the team together)        SUSTAINING (perform, then renew)
  1 Orientation      Why am I here?       5 Implementation  Who does what, when, where?
  2 Trust Building   Who are you?    ◄──  6 High Performance  Wow!
  3 Goal Clarity     What are we doing?   7 Renewal         Why continue? ──┐
  4 Commitment       How will we do it?                                     │
        └────────────── loops back on change / renewal ◄───────────────────┘
```

*Not a one-way staircase — a new member, a changed goal, or lost trust drops the team back a stage.*

## When to Use It
- You've formed a new team and want to know what to fix *first* — people and purpose — before diving into tasks
- A team feels stuck, tense, or superficially polite: diagnose which unresolved stage (usually Trust Building or Goal Clarity) is the real blocker
- Execution or commitment is weak — trace it back to an earlier stage that got skipped
- A once-humming team regresses after a change (new hire, new goal, reorg) — spot which stage it slipped into

## Key Insight
A team isn't built by jumping to tasks — it's built by resolving the *human* questions first, and the whole sequence stays jammed at **Trust Building ("Who are you?")** until someone exposes themselves first. Trust can't be scheduled or declared; it forms only when a person admits they don't know, says what they really think, or owns a mistake. The one best placed to go first is the leader: **be the first to make your mistakes visible.** That single act of daring gives everyone else permission to be genuine — without it the group stays guarded and performs as a collection of individuals, never a team.

## Example
*Elin takes over a newly merged product squad. People show up and can say why they're there (Orientation), but meetings stay stiff — nobody wants to look foolish in front of colleagues they barely know. Instead of pushing straight to the roadmap, Elin sees the team is stuck at Trust Building, and goes first: in standup she admits she misjudged the timeline on her last launch and got a key assumption wrong. That one move — the leader making her own mistake visible — changes the room. Two engineers surface concerns they'd been sitting on, trust starts to form, and only now can Elin lead them into real Goal Clarification and Commitment. Skip her own vulnerability and the squad stays polite, guarded, and permanently short of high performance.*

## Related Models
- [[johari-window]] — Trust Building runs on self-disclosure: the leader deliberately enlarges their own *Open* quadrant first (dares to be seen), which is exactly what unblocks Stage 2 for everyone
- [[prisoners-dilemma]] — a team is the ultimate *repeated* game, so the leader daring the first step is the "nice" opening cooperative move — safe precisely because the relationship recurs
- [[appreciative-inquiry]] — when a team stalls at a stage, its strengths-based "yes, and" rebuilds the energy to move on (change follows attention)
- [[john-whitmore-mode]] — the Goal Clarification stage is GROW's *Goal* step applied to a whole team rather than one person
- [[flow-model]] — the High Performance stage is team-level flow: collective skill matched to the challenge
- [[conflict-resolution-model]] — disagreement during Commitment/Implementation is expected; the six responses steer it toward consensus instead of stalling the team
- [[rubber-band-model]] — daring the first step is choosing the values-pull (build trust) over the fear-pull (avoid looking weak) — the mechanism behind the leader's first move
- [[team-model]] — the sibling assessment: before working the seven stages, sanity-check whether the shortfall is a stage the team can grow through or a real capability/fit gap — build the team you have vs. judge and staff it → see [[build-or-staff-the-team]]
- [[leading-a-team]] — theme: the leader's three jobs with a team — build it, judge/staff it, energise it
