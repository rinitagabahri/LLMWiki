# The Energy Model

**Your mental energy defaults to the past, present, or future — and that default pattern quietly shapes every decision you make.**

## The Three Types

| Type | Past | Present | Future |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Memory Driven** | 40% | 30% | 30% |
| **Dream Driven** | 5% | 30% | 65% |
| **Reality Driven** | 10% | 70% | 20% |

## What Each Type Looks Like

**Memory Driven** — You process through the past. You learn from experience, tell stories, and return to what worked before. Risk: you can get stuck in what was rather than what is.

**Dream Driven** — You live in possibility. You're energised by vision, plans, and what could be. Risk: the future stays abstract while the present goes unmanaged.

**Reality Driven** — You're anchored in now. You handle what's in front of you, respond quickly, and stay grounded. Risk: you can underinvest in learning from the past or planning for the future.

## How to Identify Your Type

You can't know your type by asking yourself directly — you'll answer with who you think you are, not who you are. Instead, observe your defaults:

- **On a long commute with nothing to do** — does your mind drift to memories, to plans and dreams, or to what needs handling today?
- **When something goes wrong** — do you replay what happened, worry about future consequences, or immediately look for a solution?
- **In conversation** — do you mostly share stories from the past, talk about future ambitions, or discuss what's happening right now?

The honest pattern is usually a surprise.

## When to Use It

- When you feel out of sync — too much anxiety (future-heavy) or too much nostalgia (past-heavy)
- When a team or relationship has friction — clashing energy types create misalignment
- When you want to deliberately shift: more presence, more vision, more learning from experience
- As a periodic check-in: has your ratio shifted with a life change?

## Key Insight

None of the three types is better than the others — context determines which is most useful. A strategist needs Dream Driven energy. A crisis manager needs Reality Driven. A mentor needs Memory Driven. The problem isn't your type — it's being locked into one type when the situation calls for another.

## Example

*Ingrid is a software engineer. Her team is in the middle of a complex migration.*

Her manager notices she keeps referencing how the old system worked — "we used to do it this way" — and hesitates before committing to new approaches. She's Memory Driven: 40% past, 30% present, 30% future. Useful when learning from past mistakes, but slowing the team down when speed and adaptation are what the project needs.

Her tech lead is Dream Driven — always sketching the ideal future architecture, energised by the vision. Great for direction-setting, but leaves day-to-day execution gaps.

The team runs best when Ingrid consciously shifts toward Reality Driven during the sprint — handling what's in front of her — and lets the Dream Driven lead set the direction. Knowing your type lets you flex it.
