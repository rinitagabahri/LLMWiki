# The Esquire Gift Model

**Decide who to give gifts to, when, and how much — using the relationship and the occasion, not your guilt.**

| Relationship ↓ \ Occasion →   | 🎂 Minor (birthday, housewarming)                                                                     | 💍 Major (wedding, big milestone) |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| 💞 Partner / immediate family | Meaningful, personal                                                                                  | Significant — invest              |
| 👯 Close friend               | Thoughtful, personal                                                                                  | Generous                          |
| 🤝 Friend                     | Modest, personal                                                                                      | Sensible                          |
| 💼 Colleague                  | Small — usually a shared group gift (everyone chips in for one present + a card the whole team signs) | Modest                            |
| 👋 Acquaintance               | Card or small gesture (bottle of wine, plant)                                                         | Small, polite                     |

The model tells you three things at once: **who** to give to, **when**, and **how much**.

## When to Use It

- Before any gift purchase — especially when "what's the right amount?" loops in your head
- When you feel guilty about not spending enough — or anxious about spending too much
- When you keep defaulting to *"however much they spent on me last time"*
- For recurring decisions (year-end / colleagues / weddings season)

## Key Insight

When in doubt, **err on the generous side — being generous beats being miserly.** A gift slightly too big is remembered as warm; one slightly too small is remembered as cheap. The downside of generosity is small (a bit of money); the downside of being miserly is a relational tax that compounds over years. The grid stops you from underspending on close people (taken for granted) AND from overspending on weak ties out of guilt.

## Example — A year of gifts

Quick run across four typical decisions:

- **Partner's birthday** → 💞 + 🎂 = **Meaningful, personal.** Not the most expensive gift of the year, but the most thoughtful. Experience > object.
- **Close friend's wedding** → 👯 + 💍 = **Generous.** A bigger contribution than usual — this is a once-per-friendship occasion. Lean to the upper end of your range.
- **Colleague's leaving gift** → 💼 + 🎂 = **Small, shared.** Chip in to a group gift (one nice present everyone contributes €5–10 toward) plus a card the whole team signs. Don't overthink it.
- **Acquaintance's housewarming** → 👋 + 🎂 = **A card or small gesture** — a bottle of wine, a plant, a candle. Don't try to perform a closeness that isn't there.

**Time-saved bonus:** the grid takes 10 seconds per decision instead of the 20-minute "what's appropriate?" spiral.

## Related Models

- [[family-tree-model]] — Family Tree decides *who* sits in which circle; Esquire decides *how much* that translates to in gift spend. Use them together.
- [[eisenhower-matrix]] — Both models fight a default bias: Eisenhower against urgency; Esquire against guilt.
- [[rubber-band-model]] — Both surface fear-pulls (*"what will they think?"*) and replace them with values-based action.
- [[status-model]] — giving is the altruistic axis of the Status Model: using money *for others* rather than the self is part of what marks the "winner"
- [[prisoners-dilemma]] — a gift is an opening cooperative move; across repeated rounds it builds the reciprocity and reputation that make trust rational, so "err generous" is a tit-for-tat-friendly strategy, not just good manners
