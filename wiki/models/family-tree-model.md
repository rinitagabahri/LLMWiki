# The Family Tree Model

**Draw your network as a tree — then read its shape to see where it's strong, where it's concentrated, and where it could break.**

```
                    YOU
                     │
       ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
       │             │             │
     School         Work         Hobby
       │             │             │
   ┌───┼───┐     ┌───┼───┐     ┌───┼───┐
   F   F   F     F   F   F     F   F   F
```

- **More branches = more diverse network.** Friends and contacts from many independent sources.
- **Boughs with many branches = concentration risk.** If one bough has 15 friends all from the same source, it's one bough — not 15. Lose the root, lose the bough.
- **Diagnostic question:** *Through whom did you meet most of your friends? Are you still in contact with that person?*

## When to Use It

- You want to understand the shape and resilience of your network
- After a major life change (job change, move, breakup) — what part of your tree just broke?
- You feel socially "rich" but vaguely fragile, like one event could empty your calendar
- Before relying heavily on one community for your social life

## Key Insight

A network isn't a list of names — it's a **structure**. The more diverse the branches, the more resilient you are. **Boughs with many branches require more maintenance and can easily break:** when the root connection moves away, divorces, or fades, everything attached to that bough drifts apart unless you actively maintain the individual connections. Most people don't see this until a bough has already snapped.

## Example — A concentrated tree

A person draws their tree and notices:
- 12 of their 20 closest friends all met through **one university friend**.
- That friend moved abroad two years ago, and contact has thinned.
- Without the connecting root, the bough is slowly dying — the 12 friends rarely meet up anymore.

The other boughs (a few from work, a couple from a sports club, family) are smaller but **independent** — they don't depend on a single root, so they're stable.

**The insight:** the network *looked* big (20 close friends) but was structurally fragile (one bough of 12 + a few thin branches). The fix isn't to make new friends in a panic — it's to **maintain the individual connections inside the big bough directly**, so they survive even if the connecting root keeps fading.
