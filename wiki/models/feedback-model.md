# The Feedback Model

**When you get criticism, decide consciously what to change, what to keep, and what to reject.**

| Reaction | When it fits |
|---|---|
| ✅ **Accept & change** | The criticism is fair, and changing serves who I want to be |
| 🛡️ **Accept & keep as is** | The criticism is fair, but this part of me is a trade-off I choose to keep |
| ❌ **Reject** | The criticism doesn't fit me — it says more about the giver or the context |

Two questions for every piece of criticism:
1. **Is this true?** (Fully / partly / not at all)
2. **What do I do with it?** (Change / keep / reject)

## When to Use It

- After a performance review, 1:1, or hard conversation
- When a piece of criticism is stuck in your head and you can't tell if it's fair
- When you're about to either over-react (change everything) or under-react (defend everything)
- Before deciding which feedback to share with a mentor or act on

## Key Insight

Criticism is **data, not verdict**. The default reactions — defend, accept everything, ignore — all skip the actual thinking. The model forces you to sort each piece separately, because feedback is rarely 100% true or 100% wrong. It also gives explicit permission to **reject** criticism that doesn't fit, and to **keep** parts of yourself even when others want them changed. Not every valid criticism requires a change.

## Example — Steve gets a bundle of criticism at work

**The feedback:** *"Your development skills aren't advanced enough, you're too quiet in meetings, and you're just not at the level we expect yet."*

Split into pieces and run each through the model:

- **Piece 1 — "Development skills aren't advanced enough."** Is this true? Partly — there are areas Steve has been coasting on. → ✅ **Accept & change.** Pick 2 specific areas (e.g. system design, performance profiling), invest deliberate time, and start writing a short *why this approach, what I rejected, what trade-offs* note before each design review. Avoid the "I need to learn everything" trap.
- **Piece 2 — "You're too quiet in meetings."** Is this true? A bit — but Steve thinks before he speaks and rates signal over volume. → 🛡️ **Accept & keep.** He'll speak up when it genuinely matters, but won't perform extroversion to fit a template that isn't him.
- **Piece 3 — "You're not at the level we expect yet."** Is this true? → ❌ **Reject.** A vague global verdict, not a concrete gap — it says more about a fuzzy bar than about Steve. Act on pieces 1–2; drop the label.

**Payoff:** One clear change, one deliberate keep, one clean rejection — the feedback is converted into a plan instead of a wound.

## Related Models

- [[swot-analysis]] — SWOT is *your own* inventory of strengths/weaknesses; feedback is *other people's* inventory of you. Cross-check the two.
- [[john-whitmore-mode]] — In the **Reality** step of GROW, feedback is a key input. Use the Feedback Model to filter it before letting it reshape your Reality.
- [[rubber-band-model]] — Both models separate real signal from noise: rubber band separates values-pulls from fear-pulls; feedback separates real criticism from projection.
- [[double-loop-learning]] — feedback can be acted on shallowly (correct the action) or deeply (question the assumption behind it) — single- vs. double-loop.
- [[conflict-resolution-model]] — both replace reaction with deliberate choice: this handles criticism received, Conflict Resolution open disagreement
