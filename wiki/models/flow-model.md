# The Flow Model

**Map where you are on the skill/challenge axis to diagnose why you're stuck — and which knob to turn.**

## The Channel

|                    | **Low Skill**          | **High Skill** |
| ------------------ | ---------------------- | -------------- |
| **High Challenge** | 😰 Anxiety / Paralysis | ⚡ Flow         |
| **Low Challenge**  | 😶 Apathy              | 😴 Boredom     |

Flow lives where challenge and skill are matched. The goal is always to move toward the top-right cell — either by raising your skill, raising the challenge, or lowering the challenge to a level you can handle today.

## When to Use It

- You're procrastinating or avoiding a task — diagnose which zone you're in
- A task feels overwhelming and you can't start
- You're bored and distracted despite needing to finish something
- You want to deliberately engineer focus on a piece of work

## Key Insight

Procrastination and boredom are both **calibration problems**, not character flaws. The task is mismatched to your current skill level. That's fixable. You have three levers: (1) lower the challenge by breaking the task down, (2) raise your skill by reading, studying, or asking someone before you start, (3) change the task entirely if you can. When possible, (3) first, then (2), then (1).

## Example

*Alexa is a software engineer at a corporate. Two scenarios:*

**Anxiety → procrastination:** She's assigned a distributed systems feature she's never touched. She opens the file and freezes. Two fixes:
- **Lower the challenge** — scope it down: "I'll just get the service to log one thing today." Start small, build momentum.
- **Raise the skill** — before touching the code, spend an hour reading the docs, a relevant article, or asking a colleague. Now the challenge feels proportionate.

**Boredom → distraction:** She gets a routine CRUD endpoint she's built 50 times. She checks Slack every 10 minutes, it takes all day. Options in priority order:
1. **Change the task** — flag to her manager: "I can do this in 2 hours, can I take on the harder caching problem after?" (best)
2. **Raise the challenge** — self-impose a time constraint, a quality bar ("zero magic strings"), or use it to learn a new pattern (second best)

## Related Models

- [[john-whitmore-mode]] — GROW helps you clarify *what* goal to pursue; Flow tells you *how* to calibrate the work itself
- [[consequences-model]] — both models push toward action over waiting; Flow reframes the blocker as mechanical, not motivational
- [[rubber-band-model]] — if you can't start a task, check whether it's a skill/challenge gap (Flow) or a values conflict (Rubber Band)
- [[maslow-pyramid]] — flow is a self-actualisation (growth) experience; it stays out of reach while deficiency needs go unmet
