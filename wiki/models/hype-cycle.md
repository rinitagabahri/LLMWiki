# The Hype Cycle

**Every new technology follows the same curve of expectations — so the "next big thing" and the "overhyped flop" are usually the same thing seen at different points.** *(Developed by the consulting firm Gartner.)*

## The Curve (expectations over time)

```
 expectations
 high ┤         ╱╲   ← 2. Peak of Inflated Expectations
      │        ╱  ╲
      │       ╱    ╲               ____—— 5. Plateau of Productivity
      │      ╱      ╲        ___——‾
      │     ╱        ╲___——‾  ← 4. Slope of Enlightenment
      │    ╱          ‾
      │   ╱  ← 1. Trigger   3. Trough of Disillusionment
 low  ┤__╱
      └──────────────────────────────────→  time
```

| # | Phase | What's happening |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Technology Trigger** | A breakthrough or launch sparks media interest — no usable product yet |
| 2 | **Peak of Inflated Expectations** | Hype explodes; wild promises; a few successes, many failures |
| 3 | **Trough of Disillusionment** | Interest collapses as experiments disappoint; the shakeout begins |
| 4 | **Slope of Enlightenment** | Real, practical use cases emerge; second- and third-generation products |
| 5 | **Plateau of Productivity** | Mainstream adoption; proven, steady value |

## When to Use It

- Deciding whether a hyped technology or trend is worth your time, money, or attention
- When something is being talked about *everywhere* — locate it on the curve before reacting
- When a once-hyped thing is being written off — is it actually dead, or just in the trough?
- To time your entry: the trough (undervalued) is usually a smarter moment than the peak (overvalued)

## Key Insight

The "next big thing" and the "disappointing flop" are frequently the *same technology* at different points on the curve. The mistake is judging it at the extremes: buying the hype at the **peak** or writing it off in the **trough** — both are temporary distortions, not the true value. The real question is never "how excited is everyone right now?" but **"will this reach the plateau?"** And not everything does — some technologies crash in the trough and never climb out. The curve doesn't predict *which* survive; it stops you from confusing noise (peak) or despair (trough) with the verdict.

## Example

*Think of **online shopping**. The web going commercial (mid-'90s) was the **Trigger**; the late-'90s dot-com mania was the **Peak**; the 2000–01 crash was the **Trough** ("the internet was a fad"); Amazon and a few survivors then climbed the **Slope**, until buying online became ordinary — the **Plateau**.*

*Why Amazon won: the failed dot-coms rode the hype — a ".com" name, no real business. Amazon bet on **fundamentals** (real customer value, patient infrastructure) and kept building **through the trough** when rivals quit. The takeaway: judge the fundamentals, not the phase; treat the trough as the buying opportunity, not the exit; and the winner is whoever survives to the plateau.*

## Related Models

- [[personal-potential-trap]] — the "Peak of Inflated Expectations" is the same overshoot: expectations running far ahead of what's actually been delivered
- [[consequences-model]] — you still have to decide under uncertainty; the Hype Cycle helps you *time* the decision rather than wait for certainty that never comes
- [[monte-carlo-simulation]] — the curve can't predict *which* technologies reach the plateau; Monte Carlo is the tool for reasoning about that spread of possible futures → see [[deciding-under-uncertainty]]
- [[bcg-box]] — a technology in the trough is a "Question Mark": unproven, but potentially tomorrow's "Star"
- [[project-portfolio-matrix]] — helps decide how much to bet on an emerging technology given where it sits on the curve
- [[long-tail-model]] — the other internet-economics model; Amazon (this page's worked example) is the canonical Long Tail engine — infinite online catalogue vs. finite physical shelves
- [[black-swan-model]] — the curve is an inductive pattern drawn from past technologies; a black-swan event is the one that breaks the pattern — the unforeseen breakthrough or collapse the tidy S-curve can't anticipate
- [[the-chasm-model]] — the adoption-side companion to this expectations curve: the Hype Cycle measures how *excited* people are, the Chasm measures who is *actually adopting* → see [[chasm-vs-hype-cycle]]
