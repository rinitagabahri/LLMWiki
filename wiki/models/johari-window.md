# The Johari Window

**A four-quadrant map of what you know about yourself vs. what others know — to find blind spots and decide what to reveal.**

*Created by Joseph Luft and Harry Ingham.*

## The Window

|  | **Others know** | **Others don't know** |
|---|---|---|
| **I know** | 🟢 **Open** — shared, visible, no friction ⚠️ *Danger: TMI (too much information)* | 🟡 **Hidden** — you know it, you choose not to share it |
| **I don't know** | 🔵 **Blind Spot** — others see it, you don't | ⚫ **Suppressed** — unknown to you and everyone else |

## The Four Quadrants

- **Open** — what's visible to everyone. The bigger this is, the less energy wasted on managing impressions.
- **Hidden** — things you know about yourself but choose not to share with others. This quadrant shrinks naturally as you build trust in a relationship — the safer you feel, the more you reveal. Some privacy is healthy; the goal isn't full disclosure, but conscious choice about what you share and with whom.
- **Blind Spot** — your habits, patterns, and impact on others that you genuinely can't see. Only feedback shrinks this.
- **Suppressed** — deeply buried material, not accessible to you or others. Can surface through crisis, therapy, or major life events.

## When to Use It

- You're getting feedback that surprises you — map it to the blind spot quadrant
- You're deciding how much to share about yourself in a new relationship or role
- A relationship has friction you can't explain — ask which quadrant the gap lives in
- You want to grow: the goal is always to expand the Open quadrant

## Key Insight

Most personal growth happens at the **Blind Spot** — not through introspection, but through other people. You can't think your way out of a blind spot. You need someone to name it. Actively seek feedback; it's the only tool that works here.

## Example

*Sofia joins a new team. After a few weeks:*

- **Open:** Her teammates know she's strong technically and direct in code reviews.
- **Hidden:** She knows she's burned out and considering leaving — she hasn't told anyone yet. She's careful about how much she reveals and to whom. *(Too much too soon = oversharing; too little forever = isolation.)*
- **Blind Spot:** Her manager notices she goes quiet in cross-team meetings and is seen as disengaged — Sofia thinks she's just being efficient. Without feedback, she'd never know this perception exists.
- **Suppressed:** Neither Sofia nor her team knows yet that she thrives with mentoring responsibilities — it hasn't come up. A new project will surface it.

The most useful move: her manager gives her the blind spot feedback. Sofia's Open quadrant grows. The friction disappears.

## Related Models

- [[feedback-model]] — the Feedback Model tells you what to *do* with input once received; Johari tells you *where* the gap lives
- [[rubber-band-model]] — hidden quadrant tensions (things kept private) can create the same pull as a values conflict
- [[john-whitmore-mode]] — GROW's Reality step is essentially a structured attempt to surface blind spots
- [[drexler-sibbet-team-performance]] — a leader who enlarges their own *Open* quadrant first — modelling disclosure — is exactly what unblocks the Trust Building stage that turns a group into a team
- [[network-target-model]] — a diverse network shrinks your blind spots faster; a mirror-network reinforces them
- [[uffe-elbaek-model]] — both expose gaps between self-perception and how others see you: Johari maps the blind spots, Elbaek the aspirational gaps
