# The John Whitmore Mode (GROW)

**Pressure-test whether you're chasing the right goal — before you commit to chasing it harder.**

| Step | Question | What it forces |
|---|---|---|
| **G — Goal** | What do I actually want to achieve? (long-term *and* short-term) | Naming the destination — and the *real* reason behind it |
| **R — Reality** | Where am I right now? What's actually true? | Brutal honest inventory of where you stand |
| **O — Options** | What could I do? What alternatives exist? | Forces you past the obvious first answer |
| **W — Will** | What *will* I do? By when? | Commitment with a concrete next step |

## When to Use It

- You're at a crossroads (career pivot, big decision, relationship choice)
- You're busy but not progressing — and can't articulate *why* you're doing what you're doing
- Before doubling down on a goal you haven't questioned in a while
- When your gut says "obviously do X" — Whitmore makes you prove it

## Key Insight

Most people leap from **G straight to W** — they name a goal and immediately start acting. The real work lives in **R and O**: being honest about where you actually are, and forcing yourself to generate alternatives instead of defaulting to the first idea. **Rule of thumb:** if the Options step doesn't produce at least 3 real alternatives, you haven't done it. The model's gift is that it refuses to let you commit until you've challenged the goal itself.

## Example — Career Pivot: Engineer → Product Manager

**Maya, senior engineer, 8 years in. Instinct: "I want to become a PM."** Whitmore forces a pause.

- **🅖 Goal:** "Be a PM." But *why?* — to own *what* gets built, not just *how*. ⚠️ The real goal may be **influence**, not the PM title.
- **🅡 Reality:** Strong technical credibility, no customer/roadmap experience. Drained by building specs she disagrees with, energized when reshaping them. Can't take a big pay cut.
- **🅞 Options:** (1) External junior PM, (2) Internal PM transition, (3) Staff Engineer with product influence, (4) TPM hybrid role, (5) Lead the next discovery sprint as a cheap test.
- **🅦 Will:** Don't jump to "apply for PM jobs." Instead: talk to 3 PMs in 2 weeks → lead one discovery sprint in 4 weeks → then choose between PM track and Staff Engineer. **Decision rule set upfront:** energized after the sprint → PM; relieved to return to coding → Staff Engineer.

**Payoff:** A 4-week experiment instead of an 18-month career bet on a goal she hadn't questioned.

## Related Models

- [[project-portfolio-matrix]] — Whitmore validates the *goal*; the portfolio matrix selects *projects* that serve it. Use Whitmore first, then the matrix.
- [[swot-analysis]] — SWOT is the natural tool for the **Reality** step. Strengths/weaknesses inventory plugs directly into R.
- [[eisenhower-matrix]] — Once Whitmore clarifies the right goal, Eisenhower sorts the daily tasks that execute it.
- [[appreciative-inquiry]] — a strengths-based cousin of GROW: both drive toward a goal, but AI starts from what already works rather than an honest-deficit Reality
- [[hersey-blanchard-model]] — GROW's "always coach" is right for only *one* of H-B's four readiness levels: coaching a keen novice leaves them floundering (instruct instead), and coaching a self-reliant expert just delays them (delegate) → see [[coach-or-adapt]]
- [[strategic-self-assessment]] — theme: GROW's goal-check is where the self-assessment sequence questions the goal itself
