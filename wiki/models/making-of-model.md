# The Making-Of Model

**To determine your future, first understand your past — reconstruct a chosen period like a "making-of" documentary, then read forward from the pattern.**

## How It Works

1. **Pick a timeframe** with a clear start and end — the last year, a finished project, from a company's founding to today.
2. **Go back to the start** of that period and walk forward along a timeline.
3. **Mark each stage** with five layers:

| Layer | The question |
|---|---|
| **People involved** | Who was there — allies, mentors, rivals, teammates? |
| **Goals at the time** | What were you actually trying to do back then? |
| **Successes** | What went right — the wins, big and small? |
| **Obstacles overcome** | What got in the way, and how did you get past it? |
| **What you learned** | What do you know now that you didn't then? |

The finished timeline is the "making-of": not just *what* happened, but *how* it was made — and what that reveals about where it's heading.

## When to Use It

- End of a year, project, or company milestone — a retrospective before you plan what's next
- Before setting new goals (feeds the "Reality" of [[john-whitmore-mode|GROW]] and the position-read of [[swot-analysis|SWOT]])
- When you sense you're repeating a pattern and want to name it
- As a team or company offsite exercise — to build one shared story of how you got here

## Key Insight

You can't honestly chart where you're going until you've reconstructed how you got here. The "making-of" framing is the trick: it forces you past the tidy highlight reel to the *characters, goals, wins, obstacles, and lessons* — the raw material. **Your past isn't decoration; it's data.** Patterns you'd never notice day-to-day (who keeps showing up, which obstacles recur, which goals you quietly abandoned) become visible in one timeline — and those patterns are the most honest predictor of the future you're about to build.

## Example

*Beatriz has just finished his **first year at a new job** and, before setting goals for year two, reconstructs the "making-of" of that year:*

- **People involved:** a patient manager who unblocked things, one teammate who mentored me, and a difficult client in Q3.
- **Goals at the time:** "prove myself and don't mess up" — survival, not ambition.
- **Successes:** shipped my first solo feature; earned the team's trust by month six.
- **Obstacles overcome:** I knew nothing about the codebase at the start — I got past it by pairing with the mentor every morning for a month.
- **What I learned:** I learn fastest by pairing, not by reading docs alone; and I'm at my best once I trust the people around me.

*Reading the timeline forward, the pattern is obvious: Beatriz's growth came from **people and pairing**, not solo grinding. So year two's goal writes itself — seek out a stretch project with a strong collaborator, rather than "work harder alone." The past didn't just describe the year; it pointed at the right next move.*

## Related Models

- [[john-whitmore-mode]] — the Making-Of produces the honest "Reality" that GROW needs before setting the Goal
- [[crossroads-model]] — answers Crossroads' first question ("where have you come from?") in far more depth
- [[personal-performance-model]] — both are time-based self-reviews: one a daily forward tracker, this a backward reconstruction
- [[swot-analysis]] — the recurring successes and obstacles feed straight into strengths and weaknesses
- [[feedback-model]] — "what you learned" is feedback from your own history: accept-and-change, accept-and-keep, or reject
- [[appreciative-inquiry]] — its Discovery phase ("when were we at our best?") mines the same past successes the Making-Of reconstructs
- [[strategic-self-assessment]] — theme: grounds the self-assessment in real history rather than memory's highlight reel
