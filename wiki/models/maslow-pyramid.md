# Maslow's Pyramid

**A five-level hierarchy of human needs that separates what you actually *need* (the lower rungs) from what you actually *want* (the top) — and shows you can't reach the top while the bottom is crumbling.**

## The Pyramid

```
                 ▲
                / \        SELF-ACTUALISATION
               /   \       growth, meaning, becoming your fullest self
              /─────\
             /       \     ESTEEM
            /         \    respect, status, recognition, confidence
           /───────────\
          /             \  LOVE / BELONGING
         /               \ friends, family, intimacy, connection
        /─────────────────\
       /                   \ SAFETY
      /                     \security, shelter, stability, health
     /───────────────────────\
    /                         \ PHYSIOLOGICAL
   /___________________________\food, water, sleep, warmth

   ↑ growth WANT (self-actualisation)
   ↓ deficiency NEEDS (the four below — felt as a lack)
```

## Need vs. Want

The lower four levels are **deficiency needs** — you feel them as a lack (hunger, insecurity, loneliness, disrespect), and satisfying them just removes the ache. The top level is a **growth want** — it pulls you forward rather than filling a hole. The trap is confusing the two: chasing a bigger *want* (status, a promotion, more achievement) when the real gap is an unmet lower *need* (rest, safety, belonging). No amount of the want will patch a hole one floor down.

## When to Use It

- You feel empty or restless despite outward success — find which *lower* level is actually unmet
- Deciding what to prioritise when everything feels urgent — secure the base first
- Trying to understand what really motivates someone else (an employee, partner, child)
- Sanity-checking a big want ("I need X") — is it a genuine need, or a want papering over a deeper gap?

## Key Insight

You can't reach for the top of the pyramid while the bottom is crumbling. When you feel restless or unfulfilled, the answer is usually **one floor lower** than where you're looking — check which need is actually unmet before chasing the next want.

## Example

*Marco lands the promotion he chased for years, then feels flat and irritable within a month. Walking the pyramid, he sees the problem isn't esteem — it's the two floors below: he's sleeping five hours a night (physiological) and hasn't seen friends in weeks (belonging). He wasn't missing more status; he was missing rest and connection. He protects his sleep and books a standing dinner with friends, and the flatness lifts.*

## Related Models

- [[rubber-band-model]] — need vs. want is the fear-pull vs. values-pull split: deficiency needs pull you *away from* fear, growth needs draw you *toward* what you value
- [[personal-performance-model]] — "have to" vs. "want to" is need vs. want; a flat "want to" usually means work is stuck serving deficiency needs, not growth
- [[flow-model]] — flow is the self-actualisation peak at the top of the pyramid, reachable only once the deficiency needs below are met
- [[crossroads-model]] — its six roads sort by need level: the safe road serves security, the wildest-dream road serves self-actualisation
- [[personal-potential-trap]] — chasing others' esteem is a deficiency need that more achievement never fills; Maslow's exit is self-actualisation, not more approval
- [[status-model]] — the egoistic/altruistic axis echoes the climb from esteem (recognition *for me*) toward self-actualisation and giving beyond the self
