# The Morphological Box & SCAMPER

**Creativity isn't waiting for inspiration — it's systematically varying the parts of a problem.**

Two structured creativity tools. Use either (or both) to generate ideas you wouldn't reach by free brainstorming.

## 🟦 The Morphological Box (Fritz Zwicky)

Break the problem into **dimensions**, list **options** for each, then combine across rows.

| Dimension | Option A | Option B | Option C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Material | Wood | Plastic | Metal |
| Power source | Battery | Solar | Manual |
| User | Adult | Child | Pro |
| Function | Indoor | Outdoor | Travel |

Pick one from each row → an instant new concept. Forces combinations you'd never freely brainstorm.

## 🟧 SCAMPER (Alex Osborn / Bob Eberle)

A 7-question checklist for transforming an existing thing:

| Letter | Question |
|---|---|
| **S** — Substitute | What can I replace? (material, person, process) |
| **C** — Combine | What can I merge it with? |
| **A** — Adapt | What can I borrow from elsewhere? |
| **M** — Modify | What can I make bigger, smaller, or change in form? |
| **P** — Put to other use | Where else could this work? |
| **E** — Eliminate | What can I remove? |
| **R** — Reverse / Rearrange | What can I flip or reorder? |

## When to Use It

- You're stuck on a creative problem and "let's brainstorm" produces nothing
- You need to generate variations of an existing product, service, or process
- In the **Options** step of [[john-whitmore-mode]] — to force at least 3 real alternatives
- When you suspect you're converging on the same 3 obvious ideas everyone has

## Key Insight

Free brainstorming converges on the same 3–5 obvious ideas because the mind defaults to nearby options. **Structure forces the leaps:** each SCAMPER prompt and each row of the morphological box is a constraint that *pushes* you somewhere unfamiliar. The constraint *is* the creativity.

## Example — SCAMPER on a traditional umbrella

Run the 7 prompts in under 5 minutes:

- **S — Substitute:** Replace fabric with solar panels → an umbrella that charges your phone while you walk.
- **C — Combine:** Umbrella + flashlight → a built-in handle light for night use.
- **A — Adapt:** Borrow from tents → an umbrella with mesh side flaps for full rain protection.
- **M — Modify:** Shrink it → an umbrella that folds into a credit-card-sized case.
- **P — Put to other use:** Flip the use case → an inverted umbrella that *catches* rainwater for plants.
- **E — Eliminate:** Remove the handle → a hands-free, wearable rain shield clipped to a backpack.
- **R — Reverse:** Flip how it closes → an umbrella that closes inward, trapping the wet side inside so it doesn't drip on your shoes.

**Payoff:** 7 concrete product ideas in 5 minutes. None of these emerge from "let's brainstorm umbrella improvements" — they only appear when the structure *demands* each move.

## Related Models

- [[swot-analysis]] — SWOT diagnoses the situation; SCAMPER / Morph Box generates the options to act on it.
- [[john-whitmore-mode]] — In GROW's **Options** step, SCAMPER is the fastest way to force at least 3 real alternatives.
- [[rubber-band-model]] — Rubber band resolves a *binary* dilemma; SCAMPER opens up *many* alternatives so you avoid false binaries in the first place.
- [[thinking-outside-the-box]] — that model names the self-imposed constraint blocking you; SCAMPER / the Morph Box is the structured method to generate ideas once it's dropped.
- [[gap-in-the-market-model]] — this *generates* many variants but doesn't say which are worth building; the Gap model is the targeting step — plot the concepts and keep the one landing in a vacancy that isn't empty for lack of demand.
- [[where-good-ideas-come-from]] — theme: this is the *method* (structurally generate variants), alongside the mindset and the target.
- [[result-optimisation-model]] — another host process for these tools: the GATHER step of each round is where SCAMPER / the Morph Box belongs — and here you re-run the generate step *fresh* across three rounds, not once.
