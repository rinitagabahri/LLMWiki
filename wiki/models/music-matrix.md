# The Music Matrix

**What your taste in music says about you — mapped on two axes.**

## The Matrix

|  | **Authentic** | **Designer** |
|---|---|---|
| **Mainstream** | Michael Bublé · Adele · Bruce Springsteen | Beyoncé · Madonna · Rihanna · Backstreet Boys |
| **Avant-garde** | Bob Dylan · Radiohead · Leonard Cohen | Pink Floyd · David Bowie · Kraftwerk |

- **X-axis:** Authentic (raw, personal, unpolished) → Designer (crafted, produced, visual)
- **Y-axis:** Mainstream (wide appeal, chart-friendly) → Avant-garde (niche, experimental, ahead of its time)

## What Each Zone Says About You

| Zone                    | What it suggests                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mainstream + Authentic  | You value emotion and relatability over image. Music as feeling.                 |
| Mainstream + Designer   | You enjoy the spectacle — production, visuals, performance. Music as experience. |
| Avant-garde + Authentic | You value depth and originality. Music as statement.                             |
| Avant-garde + Designer  | You appreciate craft and concept. Music as art.                                  |

Most people span two adjacent zones — your spread is more revealing than any single artist.

## When to Use It

- As a conversation tool to understand someone's personality quickly
- To reflect on whether your taste has shifted over time — and what that signals
- To notice the gap between what you say you like and what you actually listen to

## Key Insight

Taste is identity made audible. The matrix doesn't judge any zone — it just makes visible what your choices already say. The interesting question isn't where you land, but whether your music taste matches how you see yourself. A mismatch is its own kind of cognitive dissonance.

## Example

*Someone whose playlist is Backstreet Boys and Beyoncé lands firmly in Mainstream + Designer — they're drawn to high production value, strong performance, and emotional directness. They likely value connection, energy, and accessibility over experimentation. Put on a Radiohead album at their dinner party and watch the room.*
