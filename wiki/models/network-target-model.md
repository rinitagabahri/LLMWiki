# The Network Target Model

**The people you see most are a mirror — plot everyone you know by *type* of relationship and *how often* you see them, and the picture shows where your life actually goes and who you surround yourself with.**

## The Target

The target has **four quadrants — one per type of relationship — and concentric rings for how often you see the person:**

- **Quadrants:** Family · Friends · Colleagues · Acquaintances
- **Rings (centre → edge):** daily · weekly · monthly · yearly

Place everyone you know in the right quadrant, on the ring that matches how often you *actually* see them — daily in the centre, once-a-year out at the rim.

```
                  FAMILY    │    FRIENDS
                            │
      ╭─────────────────────┼─────────────────────╮ · yearly
      │    ╭────────────────┼────────────────╮    │ · monthly
      │    │    ╭───────────┼───────────╮    │    │ · weekly
      │    │    │      ╭─────┼─────╮     │    │    │ · daily
      │    │    │      │   YOU     │     │    │    │
      │    │    │      ╰─────┼─────╯     │    │    │
      │    │    ╰───────────┼───────────╯    │    │
      │    ╰────────────────┼────────────────╯    │
      ╰─────────────────────┼─────────────────────╯
                COLLEAGUES   │   ACQUAINTANCES
```

*The nearer the centre, the more of your life that person actually gets.*

### Then read what it shows

1. **Where your time really goes** — which quadrant fills the inner rings? (Often colleagues crowd the daily centre while family drifts out to the yearly rim.)
2. **How similar your close circle is to you** — take the people near the centre and tally:

| Dimension | Count one side… | …vs. the other |
|---|---|---|
| **Wealth** | richer than me | poorer than me |
| **Age** | younger than me | older than me |
| **Looks** | more attractive than me | less attractive than me |
| **Background** | my nationality | other nationalities |

A centre that mirrors you (same wealth, age, looks, background) is comfortable — but it's an echo chamber.

## When to Use It

- When you suspect you're in an echo chamber — same views, same backgrounds
- To check whether your circle actually challenges and grows you, or just reassures you
- When you want to broaden your horizons, opportunities, or perspective
- To reflect on what your friendships quietly reveal about your values and insecurities

## Key Insight

The target exposes two things at once. First, **who actually gets your time**: the inner rings often fill with colleagues and acquaintances while family and oldest friends drift out to the yearly rim — a gap between who you'd *say* matters most and who you really see. (Hence Don Corleone: *"a man who doesn't spend time with his family can never be a real man."*) Second, **how alike your inner circle is**: we instinctively keep people like us close — same wealth, age, looks, background — comfortable, but an echo chamber that narrows what you see. A healthy target keeps the people you truly value near the centre, and enough difference there to still challenge you.

## Example

*Lena draws her target and two things sting. The daily and weekly rings are almost all colleagues; her family and oldest friends have slipped out to the monthly and yearly rings. And the few people in the centre are all her age, her nationality, and similar income — a mirror. Together that explains a low-grade dissatisfaction she couldn't name: she sees the same kind of people constantly and the people she loves rarely. She doesn't quit her job — but she starts a standing weekly call with her parents, deliberately pulling them from the outer rim into a much closer ring, and draws one very different acquaintance nearer too. The target isn't just a diagram; it's a to-do list.*
