# The Personal Performance Model

**Every evening for three weeks, rate your current tasks on three questions — then read the *shape over time*: if it's moving, you're growing; if it's flat, you're stuck.**

## The Three Evening Questions

Each evening, rate your current tasks (e.g. 1–10) on each:

| Dimension | The question to ask | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Have to** | To what extent are my current tasks *imposed* on me? | External pressure / obligation |
| **Able to** | To what extent do my tasks *match my abilities*? | Competence fit |
| **Want to** | To what extent do my tasks *correspond to what I want*? | Desire / motivation |

## Reading the Result (after three weeks)

Look at how the three scores move across the 21 days — the trend matters more than any single evening.

- **The shape is moving / changing** → you're developing, adapting, renegotiating your work. **Good sign.**
- **The shape is flat / stuck** → your tasks aren't shifting toward you. Run the four diagnostic questions:

| # | Question | What it exposes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What do you want? | Names the target (*want to*) |
| 2 | Can you do what you want? | The gap between want and ability |
| 3 | What can you do? | Names your real abilities (*able to*) |
| 4 | Do you want what you can do? | The acceptance question — the gap between ability and desire |

## When to Use It

- When you feel vaguely stuck or unmotivated at work but can't name why
- As a three-week diagnostic before deciding whether to change role, team, or job
- At the start of a new project — to check obligation, ability, and desire are pulling together
- When "have to" has quietly crowded out "want to" and you haven't noticed

## Key Insight

The daily *ritual* matters more than any single score — it's the **movement over three weeks** that tells you whether you're growing or stuck. Good performance lives where **have to, able to, and want to overlap**; a flat shape usually means one of the three has drifted away from the others (most often *want to*). The four questions form a loop between **what you want** and **what you can do** — your performance is only as healthy as how honestly those two line up. Sometimes the fix is changing the task; sometimes it's changing what you want.

## Example

*Sam feels off at work but can't say why, so he runs the model. Each evening for three weeks he rates the three questions (out of 10):*

- **Have to = 8** — the work feels heavily imposed
- **Able to = 7** — he's clearly good at it
- **Want to = 3** — but he doesn't actually want to be doing it

*Three weeks later the numbers have barely moved — the shape is **flat**. That's the signal to run the four questions:*

1. **What do you want?** → to design new systems.
2. **Can you do what you want?** → yes — he has the skill.
3. **What can you do?** → mostly the maintenance work he's already assigned.
4. **Do you want what you can do?** → no.

*The flat shape plus that final "no" tells Sam this isn't a passing bad mood to wait out — it's a real misalignment: he's able and obligated, but not doing what he wants. So he asks his lead to take on design work. Over the next three weeks the shape finally starts moving — the sign that the fix worked.*

## Related Models

- [[john-whitmore-mode]] — GROW's Reality and Will questions echo the four diagnostics here; run Whitmore once the model says you're stuck
- [[flow-model]] — *able to* vs. the challenge, and *want to* vs. engagement; flow is the overlap this model tracks over time
- [[rubber-band-model]] — the *have to* (obligation) vs. *want to* (desire) tension is the same fear-pull / values-pull split
- [[eisenhower-matrix]] — when *have to* dominates the shape, Eisenhower helps sort what's genuinely imposed from what only feels urgent
- [[crossroads-model]] — the "what do you want?" question opens the same road-finding the Crossroads Model maps
- [[maslow-pyramid]] — "want to" vs. "have to" is Maslow's want vs. need: is the task serving a growth need, or just a deficiency one?
- [[hersey-blanchard-model]] — its "able to" and "want to" are exactly the *readiness* (competence + commitment) a leader reads to decide whether to instruct, coach, support, or delegate to you; this is the manager's-eye view of the same two dimensions you rate here
- [[making-of-model]] — both are time-based self-reviews: this a daily forward tracker, the Making Of a backward reconstruction
- [[strategic-self-assessment]] — theme: the tracked-trend diagnostic that keeps the self-assessment honest over time
