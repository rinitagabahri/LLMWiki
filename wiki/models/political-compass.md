# The Political Compass

**"Left vs. right" is only half the picture — the compass adds a second dimension (how much power the state should have over the individual), so a political position takes two readings, not one.**

## The Matrix

|  | **Left** (communism / collective economy) | **Right** (neoliberalism / free market) |
|---|---|---|
| **Authoritarian** (fascism) | **Authoritarian Left** — state controls both the economy *and* social life (Stalinism, Maoism, state socialism) | **Authoritarian Right** — free-ish market, strong state control of society (fascism, nationalism, corporatism) |
| **Libertarian** (anarchy) | **Libertarian Left** — shared economy, maximal social freedom (anarcho-communism, anarcho-syndicalism) | **Libertarian Right** — free market *and* maximal social freedom (anarcho-capitalism, laissez-faire libertarianism) |

- **Columns (economic):** Left = the community/state owns and directs the economy → Right = the free market does
- **Rows (social):** Authoritarian/fascism = the state has power over how you live → Libertarian/anarchy = the individual does

## When to Use It

- When "left vs. right" feels too flat to explain a disagreement — the real clash is often on the *other* axis
- To locate yourself honestly, versus the label you claim to hold
- To understand why two people who agree on economics can be opposites on freedom (and vice versa)
- Before a political argument — find *which axis* the disagreement actually lives on before debating

## Key Insight

The single left–right line hides the more revealing question: **how much power should the state have over the individual?** Two people can share an economic view and sit at opposite ends on authority — a state-socialist and an anarcho-communist are both "left," but one wants total state control and the other wants none. The compass separates *who controls the economy* from *how much control anyone has over your life*. Once you read both dimensions, "they're on the left/right" stops being a useful description — you have to say *which corner*.

## Example

*Diego and Fatima both call themselves "left-wing" and assume they're allies — until a debate about pandemic lockdowns splits them cleanly. Diego backs strict state-enforced mandates (Authoritarian Left); Fatima instinctively distrusts any state coercion and favours mutual-aid and voluntary community response (Libertarian Left). Same economic column, opposite social row. The compass shows they were never really agreeing — they shared the economic side and quietly assumed the social side matched. Naming the second dimension turns a confusing argument into a clear one: "we agree on the economy, we disagree on the state."*
