# The Role-Playing Model (Belbin & de Bono)

**You can deliberately change your point of view — put on a different "hat" (mode of thinking) or step into a different team role — to see what your default perspective hides.**

Two frameworks, one idea: *perspective is a lever you pull, not a trait you're stuck with.*

**de Bono's Six Thinking Hats** — wear one mode at a time:

| Hat | Mode |
|---|---|
| ⚪ White | Facts, data, information (neutral) |
| 🔴 Red | Emotions, gut, intuition (no justifying) |
| ⚫ Black | Caution, risks, why it fails |
| 🟡 Yellow | Optimism, benefits, why it works |
| 🟢 Green | Creativity, new ideas, alternatives |
| 🔵 Blue | Process, control, the big picture (the conductor) |

**Belbin's Nine Team Roles** — the parts people naturally play:

| Family | Roles |
|---|---|
| 💪 Action | Doer (Shaper) · Implementer · Perfectionist (Completer-Finisher) |
| 🤝 Communication | Co-ordinator · Team player (Teamworker) · Trailblazer (Resource Investigator) |
| 🧠 Knowledge | Innovator (Plant) · Observer (Monitor Evaluator) · Specialist |

## When to Use It
- A meeting stuck in one mode — everyone piling on criticism (permanent black hat)
- A decision where you keep seeing only your own angle and can't get traction
- Assembling or diagnosing a team by its role mix — spotting who's missing
- Forcing creativity, or forcing an honest risk-assessment, on demand

## Key Insight
A point of view is a role you can choose and change, not a fixed identity. The real power is deliberately wearing the hat — or stepping into the role — **you'd normally avoid**: the black-hat pessimist should try the yellow hat; the natural innovator should try the observer. And separating the modes stops them fighting: you argue far better when everyone is critical *at the same time*, then optimistic *at the same time*, instead of ping-ponging between attack and defence.

## Example
*Anouk chairs a product review that's dying — every idea gets shot down within seconds. She names it: the room is stuck in black hat. As blue hat, she resets the sequence. **White:** what do the numbers actually say? **Red:** gut reaction, no defending it. **Green:** three wild alternatives, no criticism allowed. **Yellow:** best case for each. Only **then** black: kill the weak ones. Separately, she notices she's the team's natural Innovator (Plant), forever throwing out ideas — so she forces herself into the Observer role for ten minutes and coldly evaluates her own proposals. That's when she spots it: the team has no Completer-Finisher, which is exactly why nothing ever ships.*

## Related Models
- [[crossroads-model]] — Crossroads names all six roads so you stop defaulting to the two you always weigh; the Six Hats do the identical thing for thinking modes — name all six, deliberately wear the one you habitually skip
- [[team-model]] — Belbin's nine roles are the named vocabulary behind the Team Model's low-to-high quality bars: when a bar is on the floor, this tells you *which role* is missing, not just which quality
- [[thinking-outside-the-box]] — that model says break the frame you drew yourself; this supplies the method — step into a hat or role you'd normally avoid and you generate the outsider's view on demand
- [[double-loop-learning]] — double-loop demands you question your own governing frame; switching hats or roles is the concrete lever that gets you *out* of your habitual way of seeing, not just noticing it
- [[conflict-resolution-model]] — both break the autopilot: get two stuck parties wearing the *same* hat at once (all facts, then all caution, then all optimism) to route a fight/flight standoff toward consensus
- [[uffe-elbaek-model]] — Elbaek has you see yourself from four fixed angles; this adds that a perspective is a role you can deliberately step into and swap, not just observe
- [[johari-window]] — deliberately wearing the role you avoid can surface behaviour sitting in your own blind spot — a self-directed complement to Johari's other-driven feedback
- [[drexler-sibbet-team-performance]] — Belbin names the role composition a team carries as it works Drexler/Sibbet's seven stages
- [[changing-your-viewpoint]] — theme: the capstone of deliberately changing your own point of view
