# The Rubber Band Model

**When you're torn between two options, name the forces pulling you to each side — then ask which pull is values and which is fear.**

```
   Anchor A  ←———— YOU ————→  Anchor B
   (stay)        stretched       (go)
```

| Pulling toward A | Pulling toward B |
|---|---|
| What's holding me here? | What's drawing me there? |

Then label each force: **values-pull** (toward something I want) or **fear-pull** (away from something scary).

## When to Use It

- You've been oscillating on a binary decision for weeks or months
- A classic "should I stay or should I go" question (job, city, relationship, country)
- Pros/cons lists keep producing a tie — and you're still stuck
- You suspect fear (not values) is driving one side of the choice

## Key Insight

Pros/cons lists fail because they treat every reason as equal weight. The rubber band forces a second pass: **is this force pulling me toward something I value, or away from something I fear?** A dilemma usually resolves the moment you realize one anchor is mostly *values-pulls* and the other is mostly *fear-pulls*. **Values-pulls deserve to win.** Staying somewhere out of fear of leaving is not the same as staying because you love it.

## Example — Move from Switzerland to Australia: yes or no?

**The dilemma:** stable life in Switzerland vs. big move to Australia.

- **🇨🇭 Pulling to stay:** family nearby, excellent healthcare, work-life balance, friendships, *"moving is scary."*
- **🇦🇺 Pulling to go:** career opportunity, partner's job offer, sunshine and outdoor lifestyle, adventure, *"escape feeling stagnant."*
- **Labeling pass:** Family, friendships, healthcare → **values-pulls**. "Moving is scary" and "escape stagnation" → **fear-pulls** (drop them). Career, partner, lifestyle, adventure → **values-pulls**.
- **What's left:** values vs. values — a real trade-off between *being present with family* and *growing my career/identity*. That's the actual choice.

**Payoff:** Stops the oscillation. The decision isn't Switzerland vs. Australia — it's *which set of values matters more to me right now*. Once that's named, pick one and commit.

## Related Models

- [[john-whitmore-mode]] — Whitmore asks if the goal is right at all; the rubber band handles the case where two goals are competing for the same person.
- [[swot-analysis]] — SWOT maps your position; the rubber band handles a binary decision *within* that position.
- [[eisenhower-matrix]] — Eisenhower sorts many tasks; the rubber band sorts the forces inside one big decision.
- [[maslow-pyramid]] — a fear-pull is usually an unmet deficiency need, a values-pull a growth need; Maslow names which is which.
- [[conflict-resolution-model]] — both surface what's really at stake beneath the surface (fear-pulls vs. values-pulls; positions vs. underlying interests)
- [[consequences-model]] — both fight paralysis: rubber band by clarifying values vs. fear, Consequences by short-circuiting the "more info" loop
- [[esquire-gift-model]] — both surface fear-pulls ("what will they think?") and replace them with values-based action
- [[feedback-model]] — both separate real signal from noise: rubber band separates values-pulls from fear-pulls, Feedback separates real criticism from projection
