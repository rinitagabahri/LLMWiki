# The Small World Model

**Any two people are separated by only a short chain of acquaintances — so your real reach is defined not by what you can do, but by who you know (and who they know).**

```
   ( YOU ) → ( people you know ) → ( people THEY know ) → ( ...anyone )

   Reaching a target:
     YOU ─ acquaintance ─ insider ─ TARGET
            (weak tie)    (bridge)   ~3 hops
```

*You sit roughly six handshakes from anyone on earth. The chain runs through your contacts' contacts — most of your reach is second-hand.*

## When to Use It

- You want something (a job, a meeting, an intro, information) and no direct route exists — ask *who is my shortest chain to it?* rather than *what can I do alone?*
- When you're tempted to cold-apply / cold-email — check your network for a bridge first
- To decide where relationship effort pays off: loose acquaintances often unlock more than close friends
- As a reminder that tending your network *is* a strategy, not a distraction from the "real" work

## Key Insight

Your reach is defined by your network, not your CV. Because everyone sits about six links from everyone else, the useful question is never "can I do this alone?" but "who is my shortest chain to the person who can help?" And the most valuable links are usually **weak ties** — Granovetter's finding that people land opportunities through acquaintances, not close friends. Your inner circle shares your information and contacts, so it adds little new; distant acquaintances are *bridges* into other worlds you'd never otherwise reach. "It's not what you can do, but who you know" — and specifically, who your contacts know.

## Example

*Kiran wants a role at a company where he knows no one. The instinct is to polish the CV and fire off a cold application — what he can do. Instead he lists his contacts and asks who might know someone inside: a former colleague, an old classmate, a friend-of-a-friend. A loose acquaintance he barely speaks to turns out to have worked there and offers an introduction to the hiring manager. The chain — Kiran → acquaintance → insider → manager, three hops — moves his application from the pile to a conversation. The weak tie he'd never have thought to call, not his closest friends, is what bridged the gap.*

## Related Models

- [[family-tree-model]] — reads your network's *structure*; more diverse, independent boughs = more distinct paths (weak ties) into the wider world → shorter chains
- [[network-target-model]] — reads your circle's *composition*; a circle that mirrors you is a short reach, a diverse one shortens the degrees to almost anyone
- [[subtle-signals-model]] — reads *communication* quality inside your network; Small World shifts the lens to reach *beyond* it — quality of ties vs. the distance they cover
- [[esquire-gift-model]] — the investment layer: Small World reframes weak/acquaintance ties as high-value for reach, not only closeness
- [[reading-your-network]] — the network theme: Small World is the fourth layer — **reach / social capital**
- [[the-chasm-model]] — diffusion is Small World at population scale: an idea "crossing the chasm" is a message propagating through weak ties from one adopter segment to the next
