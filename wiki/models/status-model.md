# The Status Model

**Read someone's real status from *how they earned* their money (earned ↔ inherited) crossed with *how they use* it (for themselves ↔ for others) — the "winner" is the one who built it *and* gives it back.**

|  | **Earned** (self-made) | **Inherited** (old money) |
|---|---|---|
| **Altruistic** (uses it for others) | ⭐ **The winner** — earned their position *and* turns it toward others | **Benefactor heir** — old money used well; noblesse oblige |
| **Egoistic** (uses it for self) | **Self-made spender** — made it, spends it on themselves | **Squandering heir** — did nothing to earn it, and it serves no one but them |

- **Columns — how you *earned* it:** earned / self-made ↔ inherited / old money
- **Rows — how you *use* it:** altruistic (for others) ↔ egoistic (for yourself)

## When to Use It

- Sizing someone up — is their standing something they built, or something they were handed?
- Looking past the *amount* of money to what it actually says about a person
- Deciding whom to respect or back: the money is not the signal — the origin and the use are
- Checking yourself: of what you've earned, how much goes to others vs. only to you?

## Key Insight

Status isn't the money — it's **where it came from and what you do with it.** Two questions place anyone: did they *earn* it or *inherit* it, and do they use it for *themselves* or for *others*. The real "winner" sits in the earned-and-altruistic corner: they built their own position and turn it outward. The emptiest corner is the opposite — inherited and self-serving, standing that was handed over and spent only on the self. So when you're impressed by someone's wealth, the useful move is to stop looking at the sum and ask the two questions the sum hides.

## Example

*At a friend's dinner, two guests have obvious money. One inherited a family business he doesn't run, drives a new supercar, and steers talk to his ski chalet — inherited + egoistic. The other, Camille, built her company from nothing and barely mentions it; you only learn later that she quietly funds two apprentices and picked up the whole table's bill without a word — earned + altruistic. Same visible wealth, opposite corners of the grid. The model's read: Camille is the "winner" — not because she has money, but because she earned her standing and points it at other people.*
