# The Subtle Signals Model

**Communication is the vital sign of a healthy workplace — so map who you talk to, how often, and to what effect, and you can see (and fix) the real shape of your working life.**

*Based on Mark Buchanan and research from the MIT Media Lab: people who communicate a lot — talking with others, reading email, mixing work and private conversation — tend to be happier and more effective.*

## The Matrix

Arrange your conversations with colleagues by **how often** you have them and **what effect** they have:

|  | **Rare** | **Frequent** |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive effect** | **Underused allies** — good for you, but neglected → talk to them more | **Core relationships** — frequent *and* energising → protect and nurture these |
| **Negative effect** | **Low priority** — draining, but at least rare → keep at arm's length | **Energy drains** — you spend a lot of time here and it costs you → reduce, or repair the relationship |

- **Frequency:** how often you actually speak with this person
- **Effect:** the consequence of your discussions — do you leave energised and clearer, or drained and stuck?

## When to Use It

- Auditing your work relationships — where is your conversational time actually going?
- When you feel drained by work despite "getting on with everyone"
- Starting in a new team — deciding who to invest your communication in
- As a manager — checking whether a team's healthiest connections are the frequent ones

## Key Insight

It's not *how much* you communicate that matters, but **who with and to what effect**. The trap the matrix exposes: most people spend the bulk of their conversational energy in the **Frequent + Negative** box (the loud complainer, the colleague who dumps problems) while their **Rare + Positive** relationships — the mentor, the sharp collaborator — quietly starve. The fix isn't "talk less" or "talk more" in general; it's to **shift the balance** toward the conversations that leave you better off.

## Example

*Priya maps a week of her work conversations. The surprise: her most frequent chats are with a deskmate who mostly vents about management — frequent, but draining (Frequent + Negative). Meanwhile a senior engineer whose advice always unblocks her sits in Rare + Positive — she only pings him once a month. The matrix makes the imbalance obvious. She doesn't cut the deskmate off, but she stops defaulting to those vent sessions and deliberately books a regular coffee with the senior engineer. Same amount of talking — much healthier effect.*
