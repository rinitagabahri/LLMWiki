# The SuperMemo Model

**Long-term memory rests on two components, retrievability and stability — review at the right intervals and knowledge sticks.**

*Developed by Polish researcher Piotr Wozniak.*

## The Schedule

| Component | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Retrievability** | How easily we can remember something right now |
| **Stability** | How deeply the information is anchored in our brain |

After learning something, review it at these intervals:

| Day 1 | Day 10 | Day 30 | Day 60 | Long-term memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First recall | Reinforce | Consolidate | Finalise | Stable ✓ |

Each review resets the forgetting curve and strengthens stability. Miss a review and retrievability drops sharply.

## When to Use It

- Learning a new skill, language, or technical concept you need to retain
- After reading a book or attending a course — schedule reviews, not just highlights
- When preparing for an exam or certification with a long lead time
- Any time you've noticed you "learned" something that disappeared within weeks

## Key Insight

The enemy of retention is the illusion of learning. Re-reading notes feels productive but barely moves the needle on stability. **Retrieval practice** — actively recalling without looking — is what builds lasting memory. The schedule only works if each review is an active recall attempt, not passive re-reading.

## Example

*Hassan attends a system design workshop. She takes good notes.*

Without SuperMemo: she re-reads the notes once, feels confident, and three months later can recall almost nothing when she needs it in a technical interview.

With SuperMemo:
- **Day 1** — reviews by closing the notes and writing down everything she remembers
- **Day 10** — does the same; recalls 80%, fills the gaps
- **Day 30** — quick recall test; it feels almost automatic now
- **Day 60** — final review; the knowledge is stable

Four short sessions. The material is hers permanently.
