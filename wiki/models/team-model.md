# The Team Model

**Rate your team low-to-high on the handful of qualities every strong team needs — the low bars tell you honestly whether it's up to the job, and where the gap is.**

The book rates the team on five qualities (y-axis = low → high):

```
                        low ──────────────► high
  ⚽ Prolific scorer     ████████░░░░   delivers, closes, gets results
  🏃 Inexhaustible       ██████████░░   tireless engine, keeps running
  🛡️ Good tackler        █████░░░░░░░   does the hard, unglamorous graft
  🧊 Strong nerves       ███░░░░░░░░░   stays calm when it matters most
  📣 Motivator           ██░░░░░░░░░░   lifts everyone else's game
```

*Illustrative profile — a team strong on attack and effort but thin on nerve and morale. The book plots England's 2010 World Cup squad this way: a line-up of stars whose profile still had holes.*

## When to Use It
- Before committing a team to a big job — check whether the essential qualities are actually covered, not just assumed
- A team underperforms *despite* obvious talent — find which quality is missing rather than adding more of what you already have
- Hiring or reshuffling — name the specific quality the group lacks before you recruit
- You're tempted to micromanage — decide whether the real gap is the team's capability or your own lack of self-restraint

## Key Insight
A team of stars isn't a star team. What decides whether a group is up to the job isn't the peak talent in the room — it's whether the *whole set* of needed qualities is covered, because one towering strength can't paper over a missing one (no motivator, no nerve). So rate the team honestly, and act on the low bar: **develop it, motivate it, or bring in the person who has it.** Then comes the harder half — Theodore Roosevelt's rule: *"the best executive has sense enough to pick good people... and self-restraint enough to keep from meddling while they do it."* Once you've judged the team capable, hovering isn't diligence; it's the failure mode. Do the judging up front so you've earned the right to let go.

## Example
*Aisha's product squad is stacked with strong engineers but keeps missing launches. Adding another senior "scorer" doesn't help. She rates the team on the five qualities and the shape is obvious: high on delivery and effort, but the "strong nerves" and "motivator" bars are on the floor — under deadline pressure the group frays and no one lifts it. The gap isn't skill, it's temperament. She doesn't hire another coder; she moves a calm, galvanising teammate into the lead role and protects the team from panic from above. The bars fill in, and — having picked the right person — she makes herself stop sitting in on their standups.*

- [[drexler-sibbet-team-performance]] — the sibling team model: this one *judges and staffs* the team (is it up to the job? delegate without meddling), Drexler/Sibbet *builds* the team you keep through its seven stages → see [[build-or-staff-the-team]]
- [[flow-model]] — "is the team up to the job?" is a team-level skill-vs-challenge check: a team whose collective skill is below the challenge sits in Flow's anxiety zone
- [[swot-analysis]] — the natural tool for structuring the honest capability read (team strengths/weaknesses against what the job demands)
- [[project-portfolio-matrix]] — its *Delegate* quadrant is the structured version of the model's second half: hand off what capable people can own
- [[hersey-blanchard-model]] — Roosevelt's "stop meddling" is right advice only at the *ready* end: hands-off delegation fits the self-reliant person, but a novice needs high-directive instructing, not trust — H-B tunes the meddle-vs-delegate call to each individual's readiness
- [[role-playing-model]] — Belbin's nine roles (Shaper, Plant, Completer-Finisher, Co-ordinator…) are the named vocabulary behind these low-to-high quality bars: when a bar is on the floor, they let you name the missing *role*, not just the missing quality
- [[leading-a-team]] — theme: the leader's three jobs with a team — build it, judge/staff it, energise it
