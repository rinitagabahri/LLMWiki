# The Chasm Model

**A map of how a new idea or product spreads through a population — and where it most often dies: in the "chasm" between the early adopters who love novelty and the pragmatic majority who don't.** *(Everett Rogers' diffusion curve + Geoffrey Moore's chasm, 1991.)*

```
 Innovators  Early Adopters │ CHASM │ Early Majority   Late Majority   Laggards
    2.5%         13.5%       │▓▓▓▓▓▓▓│      34%              34%          16%
  └─ buy on novelty / vision ┘        └──── buy on proof / references ─────────┘
                             ▲ most new ideas die here
```

*Gandhi's arc — "first they ignore you, then they laugh at you, then they fight you, then you win" — is how crossing the chasm feels from the inside.*

## When to Use It

- Strong early buzz for a product/tool/startup isn't turning into mainstream traction — and you want to know why
- Introducing a new process at work: are you still talking to the enthusiasts, or to the skeptical majority?
- Deciding where to aim limited resources — the pull to "go broad" vs. the discipline to dominate one narrow niche first
- Diagnosing a stall: adoption plateaued right after the early fans

## Key Insight

Early success is a trap, not a signal. The first ~16% (innovators + early adopters) buy on **novelty and vision** — they forgive rough edges and want an edge over everyone else. The early majority (the next 34%) buy the opposite way: on **proof and peer references**, and they actively distrust the visionaries the early adopters admire. So enthusiasm from the first group doesn't carry into the second — that discontinuity is the **chasm**, where most ideas quietly die. You don't cross it by going broad; you cross it by dominating one narrow niche until *"people like me already use this"* is true, then expanding from that beachhead.

## Example

*Tomás builds an AI note-taking app. At launch, tech enthusiasts love it — 10,000 people sign up in a week. He assumes the mainstream is next, so he spends his remaining money on ads aimed at everyone. It flops: ordinary office workers don't want a clever gadget, they want proof it won't lose their work and that people like them already use it. That's the chasm.*

*So Tomás stops going wide and goes narrow. He picks one group with a real, urgent need — paralegals buried in legal notes — and makes the app perfect for exactly them, until whole law firms depend on it. Now the next, more cautious buyers can see people like themselves using it safely, so it spreads to nearby roles, then wider. He got across not by reaching more people at once, but by completely winning one small group first.*

## Related Models

- [[hype-cycle]] — the adoption-side companion: the Hype Cycle plots *expectations* over time, the Chasm plots *adopter segments* — and the Trough is often where a product dies failing to cross → see [[chasm-vs-hype-cycle]]
- [[small-world-model]] — crossing the chasm runs on word-of-mouth: the weak-tie bridges are what carry an innovation out of the early-adopter clique into the majority
- [[long-tail-model]] — Moore's beachhead is a niche used as a *launchpad*; the Long Tail treats niches as an aggregate *market* — same unit, opposite strategy
- [[cognitive-dissonance-model]] — the "fight" stage: the mainstream rationalises away an idea that clashes with settled habits, resolving the tension by rejection rather than change
