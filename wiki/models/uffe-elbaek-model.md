# The Uffe Elbaek Model

**See yourself from four angles simultaneously — to close the gap between who you are, who you want to be, and who others experience.**

## The Four Perspectives

| Perspective | Question |
|---|---|
| **How you see yourself** | What do you honestly believe about who you are? |
| **How you would like to see yourself** | Who do you aspire to be? |
| **How others see you** | What do people actually experience when they interact with you? |
| **How others would like to see you** | What do the people around you need from you? |

The gaps between these four views are where the real work is.

## The Exercise

Rate yourself 1–10 on each spectrum across all four perspectives. Where the scores diverge, that's your growth edge.

| Spectrum | ←1 | 10→ |
|---|---|---|
| **Individual ↔ Team** | Individual | Team |
| **Body ↔ Brain** | Body | Brain |
| **Global ↔ Local** | Global | Local |
| **Form ↔ Content** | Form | Content |

## When to Use It

- Before a career change or role shift — check whether your self-image matches what others experience
- When you feel misunderstood at work or in relationships
- As an annual self-review: have the scores shifted? Did aspiration become reality?
- To initiate honest conversations — share your self-scores and ask others to score you

## Key Insight

The most revealing gap is usually between **how you see yourself** and **how others see you** — not because one is wrong, but because both are true simultaneously. The model doesn't resolve the gap; it makes it visible so you can decide whether to close it or own it.

## Example

*Alex is a product manager. He fills in the exercise:*

| Spectrum | Score | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| Individual (1) ↔ Team (10) | **6** | Slightly team-oriented — values collaboration but retains individual ownership |
| Body (1) ↔ Brain (10) | **6** | Slightly brain-oriented — ideas and thinking take priority over physical expression |
| Global (1) ↔ Local (10) | **6** | Slightly local — grounded in immediate context over abstract global thinking |
| Form (1) ↔ Content (10) | **6** | Slightly content-oriented — substance matters more than packaging or aesthetics |

Now he asks a close colleague to score him on the same spectrums. The colleague scores him 3 on Individual↔Team — seeing him as far more independent than he sees himself. That gap is the conversation worth having.
