# The Unimaginable Model

**Map your beliefs by what can be proved and what can be imagined — to surface what truly drives you.**

## The Matrix

|  | **Provable** | **Not Provable** |
|---|---|---|
| **Imaginable** | Science, facts, verified knowledge | Religious faith, love, meaning — felt but not provable |
| **Not Imaginable** | Things that exist but exceed our comprehension (e.g. the edge of the universe) | God, the afterlife, consciousness — beyond both proof and imagination |

The most powerful beliefs often live in the bottom-right: not imaginable, not provable — and yet they organise entire lives.

## When to Use It

- When you want to understand what truly drives your decisions beneath the rational layer
- Before a major life choice — to check whether a hidden belief is steering you
- When someone challenges a deeply held view and you feel defensive — the discomfort points here
- To understand others: asking where their beliefs sit on this grid reveals more than any CV

## Key Insight

Most decisions we think are rational are actually downstream of beliefs we've never examined. This model doesn't ask you to abandon those beliefs — it asks you to **name them and place them**. A belief you're aware of is one you can choose. A belief you're unaware of is one that chooses for you.

## Example

*Believing in God.*

- **Not provable** — there is no empirical test that confirms or denies the existence of God.
- **Not fully imaginable** — the concept of an infinite, omnipresent being exceeds what the human mind can actually picture.

This places belief in God in the bottom-right quadrant — the most unexamined territory. That's not a weakness. It's what makes it faith rather than knowledge. The model's value is not to challenge the belief — it's to make you conscious that you hold it, and to notice how much of your life (values, choices, relationships, sense of meaning) is quietly organised around something you can neither prove nor fully imagine.

- [[johari-window]] — beliefs in this model often live in the Blind Spot or Suppressed quadrant

- [[black-box-model]] — this model's mirror in the world of systems: as complexity grows we rely on technology we can't see into — faith replacing knowledge, applied to what we *use* rather than what we *believe*
- [[thinking-outside-the-box]] — both work by naming the invisible limit so it stops silently steering you: a self-imposed constraint is just an unexamined assumption
