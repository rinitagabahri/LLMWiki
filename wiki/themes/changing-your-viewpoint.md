# Theme: Changing Your Own Viewpoint

Your default single perspective is a self-imposed limit. These four models share one thread — **deliberately changing your own point of view** — and each supplies a different move of the same mindset.

## Models in This Theme

| Model | The move | What it dislodges |
|---|---|---|
| [[crossroads-model]] | Name *all six roads*, not the two you always weigh | The options you filter out (dream, road-not-travelled) |
| [[thinking-outside-the-box]] | Break the frame you drew yourself | The invented limit you mistook for a real one |
| [[double-loop-learning]] | Question the governing assumption, not just the action | The goal/belief you quietly protect |
| [[role-playing-model]] | Switch hats / step into the role you'd avoid | Your habitual thinking mode |

## Synthesis

All four attack the same enemy: **the single perspective you don't notice you're trapped in.** They differ in *where* the trap sits and *how* you break it:

- **Crossroads** works at the level of a life choice — you're only weighing the sensible road and more-of-the-same; it forces the other four onto the table.
- **Thinking Outside the Box** works at the level of a problem's framing — the wall is one you built; find the invented limits and drop them.
- **Double-Loop Learning** works at the level of your governing assumptions — don't just fix the action, ask whether the goal behind it should exist.
- **The Role-Playing Model** works at the level of a single line of thought or a team role — put on the hat, or step into the role, you habitually skip.

The through-line: **a viewpoint is a choice, not a fact.** Whichever model you reach for, the discipline is the same — the perspective you'd never adopt on your own is usually the one carrying what you're missing. Crossroads and Role-Playing *name* the perspectives so none get skipped; Thinking Outside the Box and Double-Loop *interrogate* the frame so you can leave it.
