# Theme: Identity Maps

Some models don't help you *decide* anything — they help you *see* where you already stand. Several models in the book share one shape: a **2×2 grid with two independent axes**, onto which you plot an aspect of identity. The grid's power is that it forces a second dimension onto something you'd normally describe with one word.

## The models

| Model | What it maps | Axes |
|---|---|---|
| [[music-matrix]] | Taste in music | Authentic ↔ Designer × Mainstream ↔ Avant-garde |
| [[fashion-model]] | How you dress | Old School ↔ Hip × Formal ↔ Casual |
| [[political-compass]] | Political worldview | Left ↔ Right × Authoritarian ↔ Libertarian |
| [[sinus-milieu-bourdieu]] | Social position / class | Economic capital ↔ Cultural capital × high ↔ low capital (status & values) |

## What they collectively tell you

- **One label is never enough.** "Left-wing," "casual," "into pop" — each collapses two dimensions into one. The second axis is where the real information is.
- **You're always somewhere on the grid**, whether you chose it or not. The model's job is to make an unconscious position conscious.
- **The gap is the insight.** The interesting question is rarely *where* you land — it's whether your position matches how you *see* yourself. A mismatch is [[cognitive-dissonance-model|cognitive dissonance]] made visible.
- **One grid explains the others.** [[sinus-milieu-bourdieu|Bourdieu]] argues taste *is* a class marker — which is why your [[music-matrix|music]] and [[fashion-model|dress]] positions leak your social position without your meaning them to.

## Which one to reach for

- Understanding *someone else* quickly, from visible signals → [[fashion-model]] or [[music-matrix]]
- Understanding a *disagreement* that "left vs. right" can't explain → [[political-compass]]
- Any time you catch yourself describing identity with a single word → pick the matching grid and find the second axis
