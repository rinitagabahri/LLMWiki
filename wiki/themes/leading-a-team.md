# Theme: Leading a Team

The *How to Improve Others* models give a leader three distinct jobs with a team: **build it**, **judge and staff it**, and **energise it**. Each answers a different question about the same group of people.

## Models in This Theme

| Model | The leader's job | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| [[drexler-sibbet-team-performance]] | **Build** the team | How does a loose group become a team? (work seven stages; the leader dares the first step) |
| [[team-model]] | **Judge & staff** the team | Is this team up to the job — and do I have the self-restraint to delegate without meddling? |
| [[appreciative-inquiry]] | **Energise** the team | How do I get a stuck or demoralised team moving again? (build on what already works) |

## Synthesis

The three aren't rival theories of teams — they're sequential tools for different moments:

- **Drexler/Sibbet** is for a team that hasn't gelled yet: resolve the human questions (why are we here, who are you, what are we doing) in order, and go first on trust.
- **The Team Model** is for the harder-nosed call: does the group actually have the qualities the job needs, or is there a real capability gap? And once you've judged it capable — *stop hovering*.
- **Appreciative Inquiry** is for a team that's capable but flat: change follows attention, so amplify strengths rather than hunting faults.

The through-line is **trust**. Drexler/Sibbet says trust has to be *started* (someone dares first); the Team Model says trust has to be *earned by judging well* then *honoured by letting go*; Appreciative Inquiry says trust *grows* when a team's attention is turned to what it does well. A leader who only builds never staffs honestly; one who only staffs never energises. The set is stronger than any one.
