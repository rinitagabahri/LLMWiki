# Prioritization

## Overview

Prioritization is the skill of deciding what to do first, what to defer, and what to drop entirely. It's one of the most fundamental challenges in personal productivity — without it, you're reactive, driven by whatever feels most urgent rather than what's most important.

## Models in This Theme

- [[eisenhower-matrix]] — Distinguishes urgent from important using a 2×2 grid to sort tasks into do/schedule/delegate/delete
- [[swot-analysis]] — Helps prioritize *what goals to pursue* by clarifying which opportunities match your strengths
- [[bcg-box]] — Decides where to invest resources across multiple bets (stars vs. dogs)
- [[project-portfolio-matrix]] — Selects which projects to take on based on learning potential and vision alignment
- [[pareto-principle]] — The 80/20 rule: a vital few inputs drive most of the output — the asymmetry that tells you *which* items on the list actually matter
- [[long-tail-model]] — The counter-lens: online, where serving the tail is nearly free, the "trivial many" in aggregate can rival the vital few — so Pareto's *cut the tail* advice doesn't always hold ([[pareto-vs-long-tail]])

## Synthesis

These models operate at different levels of the same problem:

1. **SWOT** decides your *direction* (what to pursue strategically)
2. **Project Portfolio Matrix** decides which *projects* serve that direction and grow you
3. **BCG Box** evaluates *performance* of your portfolio over time (invest more or cut?)
4. **Eisenhower Matrix** manages *daily execution* toward those goals
5. **Pareto Principle** is the *lens underneath all of them* — the reminder that inputs aren't equal, so a small slice of goals/projects/bets/tasks carries most of the return. It tells you where to point every other model.

Together they form a complete prioritization stack: vision → project selection → portfolio management → daily task sorting — all sharpened by asking *"where's the vital 20%?"*

**The counter-lens:** [[long-tail-model]] warns that Pareto's "focus on the few, cut the rest" only holds when serving the tail is *costly*. When it's nearly free (digital inventory, online reach), the aggregated tail is worth keeping. The reconciliation — Pareto governs your *effort/attention*, the Long Tail governs your *inventory/reach* — lives in [[pareto-vs-long-tail]].
