# Strategic Self-Assessment

## Overview

Strategic self-assessment is about honestly evaluating where you stand before deciding where to go. It combines self-awareness with strategic thinking — not just "who am I?" but "what's my position, and how do I improve it?"

## Models in This Theme

- [[swot-analysis]] — Maps strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats to clarify your strategic position
- [[eisenhower-matrix]] — Assesses your tasks to reveal where you're actually spending time vs. where you should be
- [[john-whitmore-mode]] — Asks the deeper question: am I even pursuing the right goal? (GROW)
- [[crossroads-model]] — At an open inflection point, names all 6 possible roads so you don't default to the "sensible" one
- [[personal-performance-model]] — A 3-week daily diagnostic (have to / able to / want to) that turns the assessment into a tracked trend, not a one-off snapshot
- [[making-of-model]] — Reconstructs a past period (people / goals / successes / obstacles / lessons) so the self-assessment starts from real history, not memory's highlight reel

## Synthesis

All six models require **honesty about your current state** before jumping to action. Eisenhower makes you admit what's truly important (vs. just urgent), SWOT makes you admit your real weaknesses and threats (vs. just celebrating strengths), Whitmore makes you admit that the goal itself might be wrong, and Crossroads makes you admit which roads you've been quietly filtering out. [[personal-performance-model|Personal Performance]] turns the snapshot into a tracked trend, and the [[making-of-model|Making Of]] grounds it in real history rather than memory's highlight reel. The pattern: **clarity before action, honesty before strategy.**

**Sequencing tip:** Start with **Crossroads** (what are my actual options?) → **Whitmore** (is the chosen goal right?) → **SWOT** (what's my position?) → **Eisenhower** (execute daily). Crossroads opens the option set; the others sharpen and execute it.
