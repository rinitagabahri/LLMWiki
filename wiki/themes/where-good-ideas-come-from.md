# Theme: Where Good Ideas Come From

Good ideas don't arrive by waiting — they come from disciplined exploration of unoccupied, unforced space. Three models cover the whole arc: a **mindset**, a **method**, and a **target**.

## Models in This Theme

| Model | Its role | The move |
|---|---|---|
| [[thinking-outside-the-box]] | **Mindset** | Break the invented limits; look in the empty space *around* the grid |
| [[morphological-box-scamper]] | **Method** | Structurally generate variants by varying the parts — the constraint *is* the creativity |
| [[gap-in-the-market-model]] | **Target** | Spot the unoccupied region worth aiming at — the vacancy a bankable idea can fill |

## Synthesis

The three answer *different* questions in sequence, and the which-to-reach-for is clean:

- **Blocked by your own framing?** → Thinking Outside the Box. Write the box down, split real limits from invented ones, and break the invented ones.
- **Need to generate options once you're unstuck?** → Morph Box / SCAMPER. Vary the dimensions systematically instead of circling the same 3–5 obvious ideas.
- **Need a market opening to aim all that at?** → The Gap in the Market Model. Map what exists and find the empty region.

They agree on the core claim: **good ideas come from exploring space that others have left empty or unforced** — the area outside the square, the combinations no one brainstorms, the corner of the cube no rival occupies.

But the Gap model adds a tension the two pure-creativity models lack. Thinking Outside the Box and the Morph Box celebrate the empty space as *opportunity*. The Gap model warns that **an empty space is not automatically valuable** — a gap is necessary but not sufficient. Some vacancies are empty because nobody had the idea; many are empty because nobody wants what would go there. So the mindset and the method open the space up; the target model reminds you to ask *why* the space is empty before you bet on it.
